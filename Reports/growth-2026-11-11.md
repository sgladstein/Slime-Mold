# Growth and starving on the big dish (2026-11-11)

Larder World phase W0 ([`larder-world-roadmap-2026-11-11.md`](larder-world-roadmap-2026-11-11.md)):
"births, upkeep and starving per walker on the GPU". The owner moved Larder
into a persistent world where crystals run out and force the mould to
expand (2026-11-11 14:06Z); the big dish had a fixed walker count, so

quoted at 100,000 walkers. All three are met below; the GTX 1070's timing

## The rule

`BigParams::upkeep` above 0 turns growth on (0, the default, leaves every
dish as it was). Each walker then holds energy, starting at `start_energy`
0.5.

- **Eating.** A fine step, after the move, a walker gains its equal share of
  the grass its cell loses: `min(grass / n, graze_eat)` with n the walkers on
  the cell, so walkers on one cell share alike whatever their order. It pays
  `upkeep` a step. The grass field already exists for grazing
  (`graze_weight`); growth turns it on even when grazing's steering is off.
- **Starving.** At zero energy a walker starves: it dies with chance
  `starve` 0.01 a step unless it eats first, so a hungry mould thins over a
  second or two rather than vanishing in one step.
- **Dividing.** A walker with `birth_energy` 1.0 or more divides. Its child
  lands in one of the eight cells beside it (a keyed draw), if that cell is
  agar and below the crowd limit; with a limit, two births aimed at one cell
  go to the lowest keyed claim. Each keeps half of what is left after
  `birth_cost` 0.2. The child keeps its parent's species, starts at age 0
  and is not sick.
- **Slots.** Walkers live in `capacity` fixed slots (0 means `walkers`), the
  first `walkers` placed alive. A walker keeps its slot, and so its keyed
  draws, for its life, which a GPU thread a slot needs. A child takes slot
  `k xor m` of its parent k, if that slot is empty, with m one keyed draw a
  step below the power of two at or over the slot count. One mask pairs
  every slot with one other, so each empty slot has at most one parent and
  no order decides who is born.

Dead slots are skipped by every rule: sensing, the move, the deposit,
grazing, conversion, contagion, respawn, the pull and every measure.

**Growth off changes nothing.** Three dishes (the nose rule; with grazing;
with two species, respawn and explorers), seed 1, 3 s: every measure line
and the dish hash match main's build before growth
([`raw/same.txt`](growth-2026-11-11/raw/same.txt), `same.sh`).

## Fed against starved

Named before the runs ([`run.sh`](growth-2026-11-11/run.sh)'s header).
**Deciding measure:** living walkers at 30 s on the nose dish (1024 x 640),
10,000 walkers to start in 100,000 slots, upkeep 0.01, grazing 10, with the
grass regrowing at 0.002 a step (fed) against not regrowing (starved),
seeds 1-8. A fed mould should end above its start and a starved one below,
and fed over starved on every seed. **Second:** living walkers at 60 s
against regrowth 0.0005, 0.001 and 0.002 on the dish at half the scale
(512 x 320, the nose rule's scent offset 18, 2,500 walkers to start, 50,000
slots), each step up paired by seed. Holm over the three.

| Measure | Result | Seeds | Paired |
|---|---|---|---|
| Living walkers at 30 s, fed / starved (start 10,000) | 96,358 (92,641-96,909) / 428 (0-9,420) | 8 | fed over starved 8 of 8, p 0.0078; fed above its start 8 of 8, starved below 8 of 8 |
| Grass left at 30 s, fed / starved | 0.12-0.20 / 0.001-0.028 | 8 | |
| Living walkers at 60 s, regrowth 0.0005 / 0.001 / 0.002 (start 2,500) | 7,113 (6,991-7,707) / 13,522 (13,004-13,841) / 25,029 (24,248-25,284) | 8 | each step up more on 8 of 8, p 0.0078; ratio 1.90 (1.73-1.95) and 1.87 (1.76-1.91) |

All three survive Holm (0.0078 against 0.0167, 0.025 and 0.05)
([`raw/`](growth-2026-11-11/raw/), `stats.py`).

What this says:

- **The mould is as big as its food.** Doubling the regrowth nearly doubles
  the living walkers (1.9 times, twice). The half-scale dishes settle by
  about 15 s and hold there to 60 s: seed 1 at 0.002 stays at 24,248-27,951
  from 5 s on, with grass at 0.12.
- **It turns over fast.** At that steady state about 8,700 walkers are born
  and about as many starve each second against 25,000 alive, so a walker
  lives about 3 s. The mould is replaced, not kept.
- **The fed arm hits its slots.** On the full dish the fed mould ends at
  92,641-96,909 of 100,000 slots with grass left (0.12-0.20), so the slots,
  not the food, stop it there. It overshoots and dips on the way (seed 1:
  88,248 at 5 s, 81,995 at 10 s, 94,955 at 20 s). The half-scale family
  never comes near its 50,000 slots.
- **A starved mould first grows, then dies.** It eats the standing grass:
  28,420-66,757 alive at 5 s, then 18,432-64,259 at 10 s, then 0-9,420 at
  30 s. The three seeds above 6,000 at 30 s still had grass left (0.009-0.028)
  and were still falling. A lean mould starves on its own, as Larder's M2
  found on the small dish.

## On the GPU

The GPU engine (`src/gpu/engine.wgsl`, `src/gpu/mod.rs`, the library engine
from PR #63) runs growth as three passes after the walk, one thread a slot:

- `life`: eat the share, pay upkeep, starve by a keyed draw on its own
  stream. A dead slot holds energy -1, which every other pass checks
  (`lives`), and leaves its cell's count. A death also clears its cell's
  claim, so a birth can claim the cell in the same tick, as on the CPU,
  which clears a claim after each move.
- `breed`: a parent at `birth_energy` whose child slot (its own xor the
  step's mask) is empty aims at a cell beside it and, with the crowd
  limit, claims it with an atomic minimum of a keyed draw.
- `born`: the winners write the child: place, heading, energy, species,
  age and sickness.

Growth runs on the outside rules' pipeline, so with growth off it is
compiled out. The six settings are `set` keys (`upkeep`, `start_energy`,
`birth_energy`, `birth_cost`, `starve`, `capacity` or `slots`).

**Twin test** (`growth_matches_the_cpu_for_one_tick`, in CI on software
Vulkan): the CPU warms a dish up 40 ticks and on to the first tick with
births (and starving, where hungry); the GPU takes that state and runs one
tick; every slot's place, heading, life and energy, every cell's count, the
trail, scent and grass, and every age, species and sickness are compared.

| Dish | Slots | Alive | Born / starved in the tick | Off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| growing, one a cell | 15,000 | 14,889 | 62 / 0 | nothing |
| growing, no limit | 100,000 | 32,744 | 12,744 / 0 | nothing |
| hungry, one a cell | 15,000 | 14,991 | 1 / 1 | nothing |
| hungry, no limit, Jenson's settings (1M slots) | 1,000,000 | 60,836 | 1 / 6,813 | energy L1 6.1e-6 |
| hungry, two species converting, explorers, contagion, respawn | 15,000 | 15,000 | 1 / 1 | nothing |
| hungry, cap 100, grazing's steering off | 1,000,000 | 61,709 | 5 / 6,782 | trail L1 1.0e-8, energy L1 1.7e-5 |
| hungry, every other walker set starving and the rest set to breed, species, explorers, contagion | 60,000 | 41,668 | 4,359 / 352 | nothing |

"Nothing" is to the bit: no walker, life, count, age, species or sickness
off, and every field the same. The two small differences are float sums
in a different order (Jenson's settings and the cap sum the walkers on a
cell). The bounds are a thousandth of the walkers off and an L1 of 1e-4.

**Planted bugs.** Twenty bugs planted in the shader one at a time all fail
the test ([`bugs.sh`](growth-2026-11-11/bugs.sh), `bugs.py`,
[`raw/bugs.txt`](growth-2026-11-11/raw/bugs.txt)): a death that keeps its
cell's count or leaves its claim, the starving draw on the wrong stream,
no upkeep, a share not split by the cell's count, half the starving
chance, the slot mask on the wrong stream, four birth directions, births
off the agar, over the crowd limit or into a living slot, a parent that
keeps its energy, no birth cost, a child not counted in its cell, a
child's heading from the wrong draw, a child that keeps a dead walker's
species, age or sickness, grass uneaten with grazing's steering off, and
dead walkers that walk. Earlier versions of the test missed five of them
(the claim, the three a child keeps, the grass); the dish with births
beside deaths, the grass comparison and the starving set sick were added
for those.

## Tried and rejected

- **Pairing slots by cycling single bits.** The first try took the child's
  slot as `k xor 2^b`, with b cycling through the bits a step. With the
  living in the first slots, most single-bit masks pair the living with
  each other, so a small mould could breed only about one step in five:
  seed 1's fed mould had 81,254 alive at 5 s against 88,248 with the keyed
  mask. Recorded in [`dead-ends.md`](dead-ends.md).

## What it means for the Larder world

- **Food sets the size.** A mould's size will track the crystals' total
  yield, nearly linearly. A world that must hold "enough for a long game"
  (owner, 14:54Z) needs its crystals' yield sized against upkeep: at upkeep
  0.01, grass regrowing at 0.002 a step over a 512 x 320 dish feeds about
  25,000 walkers.
- **Idle starves.** With food that does not come back, the mould grows on
  what stands and then dies within 30 s here (and on run-dry crystals
  sooner, below). That supports the skeptic
  review's worry for crystals that run dry: a player who waits wins unless
  the crystals hold far more than the mould can eat in a game.
- **Tubes between crystals.** Energy is a walker's own and nothing flows
  along a tube, so with food only at crystals the tubes might starve. A
  first look says they do not, but the mould eats only its roads (below).
- **Larder's eating.** Larder's crystals use the same equal shares a cell
  (`crystal_eat`, `took`). With PR #64 on main, this change merges them: a
  walker's gain is its grass share plus its crystal take, the life step
  runs after their eating, an empty slot takes no share (and is not moved
  out of a new wall), and with crystals on there is no grass unless
  `graze_weight` asks for it. Test `growth_eats_crystals`: with no births
  and no starving, the energy gained is the charge eaten less upkeep, to
  1e-3 a tick, and empty slots parked on a crystal take nothing. Taking
  the take out of the energy, or letting empty slots eat, fails it.

## A first look on Larder's crystals

One seed, not a census ([`raw/crystals.txt`](growth-2026-11-11/raw/crystals.txt),
picture `video/crystals.png`). Growth cherry-picked onto PR #64's branch
(420f360, crystals that run dry), with three changes there: the dead take
no crystal share, a walker's take (`took`) is added to its energy, and with
crystals on there is no grass. The Larder thread's world: 512 x 320, 23
crystals, rect, charge 1, eat 0.005; 5,000 walkers to start in 50,000
slots, upkeep 0.001.

- **Crystals that refill** (`crystal_regrow` 0.001): the tubes hold. A
  loose net links the crystals at 1,920-2,281 alive from 10 s to 60 s
  (tube 4,177-4,501 cells), with 79-80% of the charge left. Walkers shuttle
  along the tubes and eat as they cross a disc.
- **Crystals that run dry** (0): the mould starves with most of the food
  still there: 934 alive at 10 s, 106 at 20 s, 1 at 60 s, with 61% of the
  charge left. It eats only the cells its tubes cross; those run out while
  the rest of each disc stays full.

So for crystals that run dry the problem is reach, not tubes: the mould
needs the whole disc within reach (charge that flows toward eaten cells, a
wider eat radius, or crystals that draw walkers across the disc) before
"if you do nothing, the slime should get much stronger than you" can hold.
Sent to the Larder thread, which owns crystals.

The Larder thread answered with `crystal_flow` (charge evens out inside a
crystal, default 0.01; PR #64 at f292952). Rerun on that head, same seed and
world, crystals that run dry, flow 0 against 0.01: 1,454 / 2,297 alive at
5 s, 934 / 2,400 at 10 s, 106 / 515 at 20 s, 1 / 20 at 60 s; charge left
61.2% / 0.8%; 1,330 / 5,211 born. Flow fixes reach: the mould eats 99% of
the crystals, peaks about 1.65 times higher and holds about 10 s longer,
then starves because the food is gone. This world's whole charge feeds
about 2,000 walkers for 15-20 s, so a long game needs the charge sized
against upkeep times the mould it should become.
