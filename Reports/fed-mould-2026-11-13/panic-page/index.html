Shape C · cut off and scared

A cut-off part panics

You asked for a cut-off part to "act more like a scared animal. Either run away back to the main body or go right for the soliders. It should be more reckless", with no stronger attack. A part that has heard no news of food for 5 seconds now heads straight for the nearest soldier or building within 20 cells, or else the nearest crystal that still has food. For the first 1.5 seconds it creeps toward its target at half its pace, which is the warning you can see. Then it runs at twice its pace. A charge at a soldier or building runs for half a second and then creeps again, so a squad that pulls back in time gets away. Its bite is the same as before.

The half-pace creep is new since scenes 1 and 2 were filmed. In those films the warning was at the piece's own pace, which is already faster than a soldier walks. Scene 3 shows both versions. Scene 4 is new: the charge now gives up after half a second, and a unit that pulls back gets clear.

What to look at

  • Does the charge read as a scared animal going for the soldiers? Scene 3 is a bigger piece that gets through.
  • In scene 3, do you see the charge coming in time with the creep (right) and not without it (middle)?
  • In scene 4, does pulling back feel like the right answer to the creep?
  • It is on in shape C now, so the window plays it. larder-world -- panic=0 plays C without it.

The game designer's review: at that speed "it was over before you looked", when it should be "you saw it coming, and lost because you stayed". A cut-off walker heading straight goes 15 cells a second and a soldier 10, so even the warning at its own pace was a charge. With the half-pace creep (right), the piece pulls into a blob from about 4 seconds and slides at the soldiers for about 3 seconds before it lands. At a quarter pace (not shown) the piece barely moved before it bolted, so the warning hardly showed. 4. Pull back when it leans

The game designer asked whether a squad that pulls back when the lean starts can get clear. Not if the charge never stops: the bolt goes 30 cells a second, three times a soldier's walk. So a charge at a unit now runs for half a second and then creeps again. These films are the CPU dish, not the window. Each one is a 291-walker piece with the rest of the mould removed, so the first half second shows the old trail fading. The white square is one unit, 24 cells beyond the piece's edge, and the grey ring is its touch. It stands, or walks away at a soldier's pace from half a second in. Real time, 15 frames a second.

A charge that gave up, with nothing in reach, runs for a crystal instead: in the right film the piece turns and streams to the nearest one. The unit here is a bare point: no rock or walkers slow it and it does not shoot, so this measures speed alone.

How to beat it

  • A charge: shoot it before it closes, with enough guns for its size. With the first rule, four soldiers shot a 112-walker piece down and lost one of their own; two against the same piece both died, and four against a 163-walker piece all died. With the creep, four held soldiers shot a 245-walker piece down to 26 but died one by one; the 3 seconds of creep are the window to pull back. The charge gives up half a second into its run, so a squad that pulls back as the piece leans gets clear (scene 4).
  • A run for a crystal: a wall in its way stops it, and it bites the wall. A squad on its route makes it charge instead. If it reaches the crystal, it feeds, calms and regrows there.

What it changes before any cut

Bits the squad's own shots knock off can charge the squad too. Only a few walkers are scared at any moment: at most 16 and 24 in two CPU games on Fork, out of 13,000 to 38,000, and most of them alone. Those scared walkers make 4 to 6% of the bites on the squad (the fed body makes the rest), and the squad loses as many soldiers with the rule as without (26 and 24, against 25 and 25). Now that soldiers aim only at slime they can see, they shoot those bits as they come, so the squad loses little. In the window (Fork, seed 1, the farm hand) it has 30 soldiers at 2:30, against 33 without the rule. On the CPU world (4 seeds; idle, farm and strike hands; 5:00 each) the scores barely move. The idle hand still loses every game, and all 12 games run the full 5 minutes.
