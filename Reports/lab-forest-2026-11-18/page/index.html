Forest Floor

Can a planet's ground be simulation? Here rain falls, runs to the rivers, pools in the hollows and dries, and everything reads the same water: the slime is drawn to it and pays more to live on dry ground, soldiers wade through pools, fire reads how damp the ground is, and the picture darkens and shines where it is wet.

From the model

A run planet's ground was fixed for the round: soil kinds, a wet bit, a moisture map that only fire read. Plants, fire, char, crystals and crust were the only things that changed. The prototype adds one moving field, standing water, on the GPU engine's plant pass. Its height is 1 minus the map's own moisture, so water runs toward the rivers and wet biomes the map already has, and the rivers carry it away. Logs that rot into food are described in the report but not built.

Recommendation

Change, then build. The ground can be simulation on the GPU engine at little cost, and one field is read by the slime, the squad, fire and the picture alike. On planet 1 it is a mild tide, not a game changer. Before it goes in:

  1. Put it on wet planets (Fen, Jungle) as a planet trait, where the hollows are deep enough for pools the squad must cross.
  2. Give the player a verb on it: fire in a dry spell (fire already reads the moisture), or a pump that drains a pool.
  3. Make the water read clearly: at the full view the wet floor is a darker, mottled green and easy to miss; puddle edges and the shine need a look pass.

Next in the lab if wanted: logs and litter that rot into food where it is wet.
