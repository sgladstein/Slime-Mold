# Explosives, auto-used devices, distinct projectiles without aim, and world-state combos: how other games structure them

Research notes for the gun-system review (2026-11-16). Scope: structures, not content. Every finding carries the source it came from; things known only from memory are in the Gaps sections and marked **unverified**. Most sources are wikis and press write-ups of talks, not the talks themselves; where a wiki page is old or self-contradictory this is said.

## 1. Consumable explosives vs explosives built into the weapon system

### Takeaway
The games surveyed use three structures, and several use two at once: (A) a separate consumable slot with a few charges a mission (XCOM 2 grenades and utility items, Deep Rock Galactic throwables, Isaac active items); (B) a unit ability unlocked by an upgrade and paid from a separate resource or a cooldown (Company of Heroes munitions, Dawn of War 2 wargear that grants abilities, Mechabellum per-unit tech); (C) a passive modifier that changes every shot of the main weapon and needs no activation (XCOM 2 special ammo, DRG overclocks). XCOM 2 is the clearest case of keeping both. Element-style ammo sits in the integrated layer ("does not require you to activate it"). Grenades stay discrete consumables, and the Grenadier class gets its own grenade slot plus a launcher that amplifies grenades rather than merging them into the gun. DRG's "one throwable type per class, picked before the mission" is the closest existing precedent for "each squad carries one explosive type".

### Cited Findings
**XCOM 2 (consumables plus integrated ammo, side by side)**
- Utility items are used during missions "for offensive or defensive purposes"; most must be researched and then bought; some "have a set amount of charges or uses that refresh when you start new Missions"; "Most items must be purchased multiple times if you wish to equip on multiple soldiers." — [Fextralife XCOM 2 wiki: Utility Items](https://xcom2.wiki.fextralife.com/Utility+Items)
- Grenade types are distinguished by verb, not only by damage: Frag (damage plus armour shred), Flashbang (disorients over a very large radius, "significant offensive penalties"), Smoke (defence boost for any unit in radius, "friend or foe"), Incendiary (chance to burn), Mimic Beacon (decoy that draws enemy attacks for one turn), Battle Scanner (reveals hidden enemies). — [Fextralife XCOM 2 wiki: Utility Items](https://xcom2.wiki.fextralife.com/Utility+Items)
- Special ammunition "passively grants a specific bonus to shooter". — [Fextralife XCOM 2 wiki: Utility Items](https://xcom2.wiki.fextralife.com/Utility+Items)
- Ammo takes a utility slot (a guide calls Dragon and Venom Rounds "a waste of a precious utility slot"). A soldier can equip only one ammo type even with a second slot. The effect is automatic: "it does not require you to activate it in combat." Effects: AP ignores 5 armour, Bluescreen +5 damage against mechanised enemies, Dragon +1 damage and may ignite, Talon +20% crit chance and +1 crit damage, Tracer +10 aim, Venom +1 damage and may poison. — [Gamepressure XCOM 2 guide: Ammunition](https://www.gamepressure.com/xcom2/ammunition/za85e8)
- Kevlar armour allows "a single Utility Item"; medium armour allows "an additional Utility Item"; heavy armour can "mount a single Heavy Weapon" (a separate slot again). The wiki table is partly garbled, so per-armour counts beyond these are uncertain. — [XCOM Wiki (Fandom): Armors (XCOM 2)](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/Armors_(XCOM_2))
- Any class can carry grenades; Grenadiers get "an extra equipment slot that can only be used for a grenade". The launcher adds "bonus damage, range and blast radius on top of that normally given by the launched grenade" (Grenade Launcher +4 range, +1 radius; Advanced +5 range, +2 radius). — [UFOpaedia: Weapons (XCOM2)](https://new.ufopaedia.org/Weapons_(XCOM2))
- Weapon mods form a third layer: "Weapon mods can be replaced, but cannot be reused." — [UFOpaedia: Weapons (XCOM2)](https://new.ufopaedia.org/Weapons_(XCOM2))

**Deep Rock Galactic (one explosive type per class, chosen before the mission)**
- Each class has three primaries, three secondaries, and "a class can pick from one of four different Throwable options", carrying one type at a time (e.g. Engineer: L.U.R.E. decoy, Plasma Burster, Proximity Mine, Shredder Swarm; Scout: Inhibitor-Field Generator, Cryo Grenade, Pheromone Canister, Voltaic Stun Sweeper). Many of the throwables are not damage: decoys, a pheromone that turns enemies on each other, a stun field. — [DRG Wiki (wiki.gg): Equipment](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Equipment)
- The team resupply (a Resupply Pod for 80 Nitra, "four individual packages - one for each Dwarf") restores half of health and ammo; the page read does not say how throwables are refilled. — [DRG Wiki (wiki.gg): Equipment](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Equipment)
- Overclocks come in three grades: Clean (a boost with no penalty), Balanced (a bigger boost with an equal penalty) and Unstable (the biggest boosts with heavy penalties, "typically the most transformative"). Many "add entirely new mechanics rather than only adjusting stats": ricochet ("Normal bolts ricochet to nearby enemies up to two times"), bounce off terrain, explosions inside targets on reload, an explosion when an enemy carries three locks. — [DRG Wiki (Fandom): Overclocks](https://deeprockgalactic.fandom.com/wiki/Overclocks)
- The Engineer's grenade launcher is itself a secondary weapon with its own overclocks, so DRG has both a throwable slot and a gun that fires explosives. — [DRG Wiki (wiki.gg): Equipment](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Equipment); [DRG Wiki (Fandom): Overclocks](https://deeprockgalactic.fandom.com/wiki/Overclocks)

**Company of Heroes (explosives as abilities paid from a separate resource)**
- Munitions buy "several upgrades for your units" and pay for "special unit abilities and support powers that use munitions, such as Throw Grenade". Munitions income comes only from captured munitions sectors connected to HQ by a supply chain, so the explosive budget depends on map control. — [Company of Heroes Wiki (Fandom): Resources](https://companyofheroes.fandom.com/wiki/Resources)

**Dawn of War 2 (wargear that grants abilities)**
- Wargear items have a tier, a cost and a build time. Some grant an active ability that runs on energy rather than adding stats (Iron Halo "Grants the Iron Halo ability which allows the Force Commander to absorb damage at the cost of energy"; a Techmarine Plasma Gun "Grants the Plasma Overcharge ability"). The page covers hero wargear only and was last edited in 2012. — [Dawn of War II Wiki (wiki.gg): Retribution/Space Marines/Wargear](https://dawnofwar2.wiki.gg/wiki/Retribution/Space_Marines/Wargear)

**Mechabellum (per-unit-type tech in an auto-battler)**
- Tech is bought per unit type, and each extra tech on one type costs 200 more ("all four techs on a unit comes with a total 1200-supply premium"). Techs change behaviour: Fortress barrier and anti-air missiles, a Fang-summoning tech, Fang personal shield, a Mustang missile interceptor, Melting Point "energy diffraction" against swarms, and Hacker restoring a hacked Rhino to full health. The player places units before the battle and only watches during it. — [GamePretty: Mechabellum Beginners' Guide](https://gamepretty.com/mechabellum-beginners-guide/)

**Isaac and Risk of Rain 2 (a single active slot next to many passives)**
- Isaac: "only one activated item can be held at a time" (unless Schoolbag). A new pickup swaps the old one onto a pedestal. Most actives recharge "either by clearing rooms or recharging over time", some by shooting, some are single use or cost coins or health; "Some items are passive, which add an effect permanently to Isaac, while others only activate when used." — [Binding of Isaac Rebirth Wiki: Active item](https://bindingofisaacrebirth.wiki.gg/wiki/Active_item)
- Risk of Rain 2: Gesture of the Drowned cuts equipment cooldown by 50% and "Forces your Equipment to activate whenever it is off cooldown". — [RogueRanker: Gesture of the Drowned](https://rogueranker.com/risk-of-rain-2-gesture-of-the-drowned/)

**Helldivers 2**
- The wiki weapons index lists throwables in their own Standard and Special tabs, separate from support weapons, which are summoned "by inputting a unique stratagem code". Grenades are told apart by armour penetration (G-6 Frag Medium, G-12 High Explosive Heavy) and by type (incendiary, smoke). — [Helldivers Wiki (wiki.gg): Grenades / weapons index](https://helldivers.wiki.gg/wiki/Grenades)
- Arrowhead CEO Johan Pilestedt, per a headline: new stratagems get made because "we play the game and get bored by the current selection so we add another one" (headline only, article not read). — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/helldivers-2-boss-explains-how-new-stratagems-get-made-we-play-the-game-and-get-bored-by-the-current-selection-so-we-add-another-one)

### Inferences
- **Where each structure fits.** Integrated, always-on modifiers (XCOM ammo, DRG overclocks) suit effects whose value doesn't depend on timing: they ride every shot and need no decision. Consumables and abilities suit effects whose value depends on *where and when*: smoke, a decoy, a scanner, a wall breach. XCOM puts its element-style effects (burn, poison) in the integrated ammo layer and keeps its *positional* effects (smoke, decoy, reveal, frag) as consumables. A rule for this game could follow the same line. Timing-free field writes (a little burn on every hit, contagion seeding) belong in gun cards. Placement-dependent devices (a crater wall across a vein, a sleep-gas cloud held in a gully, a charge on a wall) stay player-triggered.
- **One explosive type per squad has a precedent**, and DRG's version has three parts worth copying. The choice is made before the mission. The options in a slot differ by verb (damage, decoy, mine, stun field, pheromone). The count is small and refilled through a shared, visible team resupply. It gives each squad an identity without adding a decision per throw.
- **XCOM's Grenadier pattern is a halfway house.** The explosive stays a consumable, but one unit type gets a dedicated slot plus a launcher that changes range and radius. Here that could mean a gun card that *amplifies* a squad's carried device (longer throw, bigger crater) without turning the device into bullets.
- **Separate resource or bank?** CoH ties explosives to a resource earned from map control (munitions sectors). This game's bank is paid by crystal pumps, so "explosives cost bank" already ties them to map control the same way. That is an argument that the current bank-paid tool system follows a proven RTS structure, not an oddity.
- **Decision density at RTS scale.** Every structure that survives at squad scale limits active decisions: one active (Isaac), one ammo type (XCOM), one throwable type (DRG), one autocast at a time (WC3, see section 2). None of the sources gives every unit several manual consumables at RTS scale.

### Gaps
- No primary design commentary (GDC talk, developer interview) on *why* XCOM 2 split ammo from grenades or gave Grenadiers the launcher was found; the XCOM 2 design talk the brief suggested was not located.
- Helldivers 2 loadout specifics are **unverified (from memory)**: one primary, one secondary, one grenade type and four stratagems per player; stratagems on cooldowns, some with limited uses; grenades refilled from supply packs or ammo boxes; an armour passive that adds grenades. The wiki page fetched did not confirm them.
- Company of Heroes details beyond munitions costs are **unverified (from memory)**: grenades and satchels unlocked per squad by tech or upgrade, each with a munitions price and a cooldown after use.
- Jagged Alliance 3, Phoenix Point, Door Kickers 2 (the guide fetch failed) and Starship Troopers: Terran Command (the guide fetch failed) were not verified. From memory, **unverified**: in Phoenix Point and JA3 grenades are inventory items thrown with action points; in Door Kickers 2 grenades are equipment slots the player orders thrown; in Terran Command grenades and similar are squad abilities on cooldowns.
- No Reddit or Steam threads were found that state the decision-density, micro, resource-vs-cooldown or AI-auto-use trade-off directly; the inferences above come from structures, not from quoted player debate.

## 2. Autocast and AI use of abilities by units the player doesn't micro

### Takeaway
Autocast systems share four properties. Autocast is a per-ability toggle. It is suppressed while the unit carries out a direct order but survives attack-move and hold. Usually only one autocast ability can be active at a time. Autocast and manual use behave differently in small but important ways. The main documented cost is losing timing control: Risk of Rain 2's forced-autocast item advises using only equipment "where constant firing is always useful". Larian found that AI-controlled crowd control became "a dominant tactic" and had to be cut back. Riot found that creative, niche items created dependency and balance trouble, and moved that creativity into a separate pool.

### Cited Findings
- Warcraft 3: "Autocast abilities can be set to automatically cast" and they "change a unit's behaviour and can cause it to move in order to cast their spell". The toggle is a right-click on the ability. "Only one arrow and one autocast ability can be active at the same time." Under a direct order the unit "usually prioritizes this order and does not cast the autocast ability until the order is finished", while Attack-Move, Patrol, Stop and Hold Position do not disable autocast. Examples include Acolyte Restore, Banshee Curse, Crypt Fiend Web, Dark Wizard Raise Dead and Moon Well Replenish. — [Liquipedia Warcraft: Autocast](https://liquipedia.net/warcraft/Autocast)
- Dota 2: attack modifiers "apply an effect to a unit's basic attacks", and most "can be set to Autocast", which makes "each attack to apply the effect if the hero can support their costs". Autocast uses attack range, manual cast uses cast range. Manual casting "neither draws aggro from lane creeps nor towers", while autocast counts as a regular attack. Priority runs manual active modifier > autocast modifier > item modifier. If the attack misses, the modifier fails too. — [Dota 2 Wiki (Fandom): Attack Modifier](https://dota2.fandom.com/wiki/Attack_Modifier)
- Risk of Rain 2, Gesture of the Drowned: "You lose manual control of your equipment entirely". For example, a defensive item "fires as soon as it's ready, even if you're about to take a big hit"; the page advises picking equipment where constant firing is always useful rather than situational ones, and notes that "Dropping or swapping Gesture mid-run re-enables manual control." — [RogueRanker: Gesture of the Drowned](https://rogueranker.com/risk-of-rain-2-gesture-of-the-drowned/)
- Divinity: Original Sin 2, Swen Vincke on enemy AI: "Our combat designers had to remove crowd control from the AI"; "one guy was dealing with everything, but it was a dominant tactic"; "So we had to nerf it a lot." The article also says, unattributed, that "the AI started teaching players new mechanics". — [PC Gamer: The making of Divinity: Original Sin 2](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-making-of-divinity-original-sin-2)
- TFT (auto-battler, items combine from components): Riot narrowed the gap between "best in slot" items and the rest by giving items "a bunch of well-rounded stats". It called the niche Rapid Firecannon "a balance nightmare" that "vastly empowered specific melee-carries to the point of dependency upon the item". Core items had become "a bit stat heavy and less creative", so creative effects move to a separate Artifacts pool, which "allow us to do very cool things that our core item system does not allow for". — [Riot dev blog: Teamfight Tactics Remix Rumble learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-teamfight-tactics-remix-rumble-learnings/)
- Mechabellum's per-unit techs (barrier, interceptor, summons) are used by units the player cannot command once battle starts; the player's lever is choosing the tech and placing units before battle. — [GamePretty: Mechabellum Beginners' Guide](https://gamepretty.com/mechabellum-beginners-guide/)

### Inferences
- In this game the squads are already on a kind of permanent attack-move. WC3's rule (autocast survives attack-move, is paused by a direct order) is a clean default for any squad-carried device: it auto-uses while the squad fights and holds while the player is moving it.
- RoR2's advice gives a test for which devices may auto-fire: only those whose use is good almost whenever they come off cooldown. Most of this game's devices change the map in one place (a crater wall, a gas cloud held by walls, a fire line), so they fail that test. That argues for keeping them player-placed, or for an auto-use that only fires on a narrow, readable trigger ("throw when slime is within X of the squad").
- Larian's crowd-control finding warns that an AI throwing control effects (sleep gas, stun) without limit can dominate. If soldiers auto-throw sleep gas, its total power needs a cap, such as one charge per squad, and the cap should be visible on screen.
- Riot's split, a "stat" core for reliability plus a separate pool for creative, game-changing effects, maps onto this game's gun cards versus a separate device or "artifact" layer. It is a deliberate structure, not a failure to integrate.
- Dota's manual-vs-autocast differences (range, aggro) show that a game can make a manual order slightly better than the automatic version, which rewards micro without requiring it.

### Gaps
- No StarCraft source was fetched. From memory, **unverified**: StarCraft II keeps few autocast abilities (the Medivac's heal is automatic), and the series leans on manual casting for skill expression.
- No developer commentary on autocast readability complaints (players not knowing why a unit cast or didn't cast) was found.
- TFT's component-combination rule (two components make one completed item) is **unverified from a fetched source (from memory)**; the Riot blog read did not describe it.

## 3. Distinct projectiles when the player does not aim; homing

### Takeaway
With automatic aim, weapons get their identity from the **targeting rule and the motion pattern**, not from damage. Vampire Survivors' base weapons each name one: nearest enemy, random enemy, facing direction, horizontal sweep, boomerang, orbit, bouncing, ground zones, an aura around the player. Global passives then scale all weapons along shared axes (count, area, speed, duration). Bloons TD 6's top-tier upgrades change the *form* of a tower's attack (tacks become a fire ring or spinning blades). Homing in auto-aim or aim-assisted games is framed as a trade: guidance for damage, volume or precision. Saros (2026) treats homing as "a weapon identity layer", and DRG offers guided against unguided overclocks on the same launcher.

### Cited Findings
- Vampire Survivors base weapons, from their in-game descriptions: Whip "Attacks horizontally, passes through enemies"; Magic Wand "Fires at the nearest enemy"; Knife "Fires quickly in the faced direction"; Cross "Aims at nearest enemy, has boomerang effect"; King Bible "Orbits around the character"; Fire Wand "Fires at a random enemy, deals heavy damage"; Garlic "Damages nearby enemies"; Santa Water "Generates damaging zones"; Runetracer "Passes through enemies, bounces around"; Lightning Ring "Strikes at random enemies"; Peachone "Bombards in a circling zone". Passives that change all weapons: Duplicator "Weapons fire more projectiles", Candelabrador area +10%, Bracer projectile speed +10%, Spellbinder duration +10%. — [gameplay.tips: Vampire Survivors Ultimate Basic Guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/vampire-survivors-ultimate-basic-guide-weapons-passive-boosts-characters-etc.html)
- Vampire Survivors evolution: a base weapon at level 8 (in most cases) plus a specific paired passive, after surviving 10 minutes, yields a chest with the evolved weapon. Evolutions change behaviour, not just numbers: Holy Wand "No delay on firing", Unholy Vespers "Continuous projectiles", Hellfire "Passes through enemies", Bloody Tear "Deals critical damage and absorbs XP". — [PC Gamer: How to get all the evolved weapons in Vampire Survivors](https://pcgamer.com/vampire-survivors-evolve-weapons-evolutions-guide)
- BTD6 Tack Shooter: each path changes the attack's form. Path 1 replaces the tacks with a fire area (Ring of Fire), then Inferno Ring, which fires every 0.1 s and "adds a meteor attack that seeks targets on screen". Path 2 replaces tacks with large high-pierce blades (Blade Shooter), then adds an ability firing blades "rapidly in all directions" (Blade Maelstrom, Super Maelstrom). Path 3 is "Many, many tacks" (Tack Zone, 16 to 32 tacks a shot, extra damage to MOAB-class). Path 1's fire ring "cannot pop Purple Bloons", a built-in counter. — [Bloons Wiki: Tack Shooter (BTD6)](https://www.bloonswiki.com/Tack_Shooter_(BTD6))
- BTD6 Dart Monkey path 3 at tier 3 swaps darts for a long-range crossbow ("can pop 3 layers of Bloon for every hit"); crosspath upgrades from other paths then add pierce or rate (e.g. 2-0-3, 0-2-3). — [Bloons Wiki: Crossbow (BTD6)](https://www.bloonswiki.com/Crossbow_(BTD6))
- Saros (Housemarque, 2026), per a guide: Autohit is "generous homing, not target lock", a per-weapon property flagged by "a visible icon on the weapon display". Autohit-disabled variants reportedly do "more damage per bullet than their homing counterparts", homing aims at centre mass and so misses weak points, and homing lets players spend attention on movement. The guide calls it "a weapon identity layer" and "designed so both answers can be correct", but admits its weapon-by-weapon details are uncertain. — [FinalBoss: Saros: How Autohit Works](https://finalboss.io/saros-how-autohit-works-homing-vs-manual-aim)
- DRG homing trade-offs: the Engineer's LOK-1 Smart Rifle "Seeker Rounds" makes "Locked shots will always hit their target even finding their way around heavy armor". The Gunner's guided rocket launcher has one overclock that raises turn rate and others that remove guidance for volume ("Rocket Barrage": "Unleash a torrent of small unguided rockets"). — [DRG Wiki (Fandom): Overclocks](https://deeprockgalactic.fandom.com/wiki/Overclocks)
- DRG's LOK-1 overclock "Explosive Chemical Rounds": "Every enemy with 3 locks triggers an explosion on the last shot", so the targeting state itself becomes the trigger. — [DRG Wiki (Fandom): Overclocks](https://deeprockgalactic.fandom.com/wiki/Overclocks)

### Inferences
- **A targeting rule is a card in its own right.** In a game where soldiers aim for you, "nearest", "random", "strongest", "where trail is densest", "the sickest walker", "the cut-off fragment" and "along the vein" are distinct verbs that cost almost nothing to build and can be read on screen from where the shots land. Vampire Survivors shows a whole weapon roster can be told apart by targeting and motion alone.
- **Homing is worth having only if it changes what gets hit, not whether it hits.** Auto-aim already handles accuracy. Here a seeker card would differ if it seeks a *field value* (follow the trail gradient into a vein, home on scent, curve toward the alarm field, chase panicking fragments) and so lands somewhere the soldier's line of fire wouldn't. Saros and DRG both pair homing with a cost: less damage per hit, a lower rate, or giving up volume.
- **BTD6 shows the "same gun, entirely different form" ladder.** High tiers swap the projectile's form (a ring, a blade, a crossbow) and some add a deliberate blind spot (fire can't hit one bloon type). For this game, gun cards that change the *form* of a shot (a disc becomes a line, a ring or a lingering zone) would read more clearly at RTS zoom than cards that change size.
- **Evolution needs two named inputs.** Vampire Survivors' evolutions require a specific weapon plus a specific passive. That gives a luck-driven but discoverable "combo unlocks a new behaviour" structure that is not "more damage".

### Gaps
- No developer commentary (Poncle, Ninja Kiwi) on *why* these targeting rules make weapons feel distinct was found; the inference comes from the structure of the rosters.
- Kingdom Rush was not researched. From memory, **unverified**: four tower families, each splitting into two specialised towers at the top tier with purchasable abilities.
- No game-design essay on homing in auto-aim games specifically (e.g. GMTK) was found; the Saros guide is a third-party guide written soon after release and says it is uncertain.
- The BTD6 crosspathing rule (one path to tier 5, a second to tier 2) is **unverified (from memory)**; the wiki pages read show combinations such as 5-2-0 but don't state the rule.

## 4. Combos built from world or element state

### Takeaway
The most-cited structure is BotW's chemistry engine. It has three rules: elements change materials, elements change each other, materials don't change each other. The rules apply everywhere, which is what makes them "multiplicative". Genshin Impact formalises "two inputs": the first element becomes an aura on the target, the second triggers a reaction that uses up gauge. Some elements can only trigger, and an internal cooldown limits how often one source can apply. DOS2 shows the payoff and the cost of open surface systems: "If you can think it up, it should work", alongside "There was always something broken". Into the Breach sets clarity as the limit: "we would sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time." Noita adds that simulation can get in the way of play.

### Cited Findings
**BotW chemistry engine**
- Fujibayashi's "multiplicative gameplay": the goal was objects that react to the player and to each other. The prototype had one rule ("there's a simple goal, can you reach it?"), and multiplying actions, items, landscape and reactive objects under that rule produced many events with player-found solutions. — [Thumbsticks: GDC 17, Breaking conventions with Breath of the Wild](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-breaking-conventions-breath-of-the-wild)
- Dohta (GDC 2017): "Elements" have no constant state (fire, water, ice, electricity, wind); "materials" are solid objects (trees, rocks, the player). Rules: "Elements can change the state of materials." "Elements are able to change each other's states." "Materials cannot influence each other's states." A physics engine is a rule-based *movement* calculator; the chemistry engine is a rule-based *state* calculator; "Aside from rigid body dynamics we consider all phenomena that occur in nature to be chemistry." Elements "can also generate energy and impact the physical world". He also said puzzles that "reference the way the real world works can help players solve them instinctively." — [Thumbsticks: GDC 17, Breath of the Wild, science and lies](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-breath-of-the-wild-science-lies)
- A secondary write-up stresses universality ("There's no special logic for 'this fire interacts with this grass *only in this shrine*'"; "Burn grass anywhere in Hyrule, get an updraft anywhere in Hyrule") and lists examples: ice freezes water, fire melts ice, water puts out fire, fire heats air into an updraft. — [saschb2b game-mechanics notes: BotW chemistry engine](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/zelda-botw/chemistry-engine)

**Genshin Impact (aura plus trigger)**
- If a target is "not affected by any Element", the next element applies an aura, a gauge that decays or is used up. A later element "will act as an Elemental trigger for a reaction". Anemo and Geo cannot make auras ("Players can only use these elements as a trigger"). Gauges come in 1U/2U/4U with set decay times. Transformative reactions subtract the trigger's units from the aura. Amplifying reactions use 2.0x/0.5x unit modifiers depending on which element is the trigger. An internal cooldown per enemy means a source within it "WILL NOT apply Elemental aura/trigger" while still doing damage. — [KQM Theorycrafting Library: Elemental Gauge Theory](https://library.keqingmains.com/evidence/combat-mechanics/elemental-effects/elemental-gauge-theory)

**Magicka (mixing at the caster)**
- Eight base elements plus two combined ones (steam from water and fire, ice from water and cold); up to five chained per cast; order doesn't matter except for named Magicks; opposing elements "cannot be combined together due to their opposing nature (for instance, fire and cold)". The same mix can be cast four ways: projectile or beam, area, weapon imbue, or on oneself. — [Wikipedia: Magicka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magicka)

**Divinity: Original Sin 2 (surfaces)**
- Systems designer Nick Pechenin: the arena prototype tested "what new elemental combinations we could come up with"; "We knew it had to be deep, with a lot of systems and a lot of combinations that we'll probably never see"; early tests had "lava surfaces everywhere"; open-systems principle "If you can think it up, it should work." The article describes combos such as rain making puddles that are then blessed, electrified water, and oil and poison puddles that explode. Combat designer Edouard Imbert: "It has to be spicy or it's boring"; "If you're only killing enemies and dealing damage without thinking, you're not engaged"; he tested one fight 12 to 15 times, spent ten days on the final boss, and "There was always something broken." — [PC Gamer: The making of Divinity: Original Sin 2](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-making-of-divinity-original-sin-2)

**Deep Rock Galactic (status-conditional weapon effects)**
- "Bullets of Mercy": "Deal significantly more damage to targets that are burning, stunned, electrocuted, frozen, poisoned, etc." "Volatile Bullets": bonus damage against burning targets with lower base damage. One weapon's effect depends on a state another weapon or teammate set up. — [DRG Wiki (Fandom): Overclocks](https://deeprockgalactic.fandom.com/wiki/Overclocks)

**Into the Breach (clarity as the limit)**
- Justin Ma: "As a game design principle, we would sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time"; "If it can be shown in a clear way, [then] yes, we can have that." The clarity requirement "restricted our game design options considerably." On pushes: "You could type out a hundred times, 'Damages a tile and pushes adjacent tiles,' but showing that little animation of them moving is a thousand times more effective". Animated tooltips were "the most important decision we made about this." — [Game Developer: Into the Breach dev on UI design](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-)

**Noita (simulated materials)**
- Petri Purho: the simulation "functions as this emergence-generating machinery that keeps on generating these interesting situations of play"; "There's always something new to be discovered, even for us"; an anecdote in which an enemy's falling body broke an oil lantern and "the oil spilled on me and I was set ablaze. That had never happened to me before." He also warned that "The simulation can, in fact, be in the way of more interesting gameplay" (collapsing buildings left "just a bunch of rubble at the bottom of the screen"), and that play demands "a high degree of environmental awareness". — [Game Developer: Road to the IGF, Nolla Games' Noita](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-nolla-games-i-noita-i-)

### Inferences
- **BotW's three rules map onto this simulation almost directly.** The fields (scent, sleep gas, contagion, fire, moisture, alarm, no-trail ground) are "elements". Walkers, plants, terrain and soldiers are "materials". The useful constraint is rule 3: materials don't change each other, so every combo goes through a field. That is already how this simulation works (walkers only interact through trail and fields). It fits the owner's rule that the picture *is* the data.
- **Genshin's aura-and-trigger split is a template for "a reaction needs two inputs".** One card or device writes a lasting state (an "aura": wet ground, a sick patch, a sleeping cluster, no-trail ground). A second, different input triggers it. Three of its details carry over. Trigger-only elements (Anemo, Geo) give every gun a role in combos even if it can't set one up. Gauge decay creates a timing window. The per-source internal cooldown stops one gun firing fast from both setting up and triggering a combo, which keeps two-input combos genuinely two-input (two squads or two cards).
- **Magicka's "opposing elements cancel" rule** is a cheap way to make some combinations *fail* readably (water already stops fire in this simulation; sleep might cancel alarm), which keeps chaos legible.
- **DOS2 shows the cost of open systems.** Combos the designers "will probably never see" come with "There was always something broken". Under the owner's "build first, balance later" rule this is acceptable, but it argues for cheap smoke checks per combo, not exhaustive balance.
- **Into the Breach's push and Noita's warning both say the same thing:** a world-state combo is only good if the player can see it happen. At RTS zoom with auto-fire, a combo should leave a mark in a field (a scorched ring, a gas plume, a sick-green patch) that is visible on its own, not a damage number.
- **DRG's status-conditional bullets** are a cheap integrated version of a combo: a gun card that does something extra to walkers already in state X (asleep, sick, burning, panicking, on no-trail ground) turns two squads' cards into a two-input reaction with no new device.
