Mould looks pass

Does the mould game look better? Every pair below is the same dish at the same moment, drawn the old way (left) and the new way (right). Nothing about how the mould behaves changed, and everything new on screen is something the dish really holds.

Updated after the independent review. The first version of this pass is already in the game. A second reviewer then found five things to fix, and the pictures below now show the fixed drawing: hungry tube goes grey again, the stain is fainter, the lamp is warm light, food flow is brighter, and the dish edge is smooth where a tube meets it.

Seed 1, the scripted plan hand. A salt wall goes down at 25 s, a snip at 35 s, and the lamp is held from 44 s. Scale 4, the game's own look. Thinner tubes are left out because they change how the mould behaves, so they stay with the mould thread.

Newest: the mould drawn straight from the simulation

You picked this on the review's card. It is now the default in the game, and F7 flips back to the tube look for comparison. Left is the tube look, right is the new one, on the same dish at the same moment.

  • No cut-off edge. Brightness is the trail itself through one fixed curve, so a strand fades out the way its numbers do. Strands read thinner, with no change to how the mould behaves.
  • No fed/unfed line. Colour slides from gold to grey by how long since food reached that part, spread along the strand. With food flow on it is the food the tube holds.
  • Every walker is a speck of light, so busy places are brighter because more walkers are there.
  • The same for everything else: the wild mould glows violet, sleeping mould is a dark amber crust, and the food piles are drawn on top so you can still read them. The rim cuts the light along its smooth curve.

New: water and sleeping mould

Keep It Alive now has water. The lamp dries the ground, mould on dry ground falls asleep, and a drop of water wakes it. Until now the game drew sleeping mould as grey starving tube, drew no wet or dry ground, and had no water tile on the bar. Left is how it looked with the water code alone, right is the new drawing.

  • Sleeping mould is a brown crust, matte and unlit, like the real mould's dried resting form. It is drawn from the walkers that really are asleep.
  • Wet ground is darker and blue where a drop landed, and dried ground is pale and dusty where the lamp has been. Both come from the dish's own wetness.
  • The water tool has its own tile with a drop icon, its drops and the next one refilling. Before, the bar showed "Not here" where it should be.
  • Before you click, the drop shows the ground it will soak and says how many sleepers it will wake, lighting each one. With none left it says when the next drop comes.

What changed

  • You can see the walkers. Every real walker is a small grain at its true position each frame. They stream along the tubes and crowd at the food. F4 switches them between grains, glints and off.
  • Food has one colour ramp, white-gold where it is rich through gold to amber. Tube that is cut off from food turns cold grey the moment it goes hungry, as before. With food flow on, the colour is the food the tube really carries, going grey where it carries almost none. F3
  • No more 4-pixel steps. The dish rim, the inside of every tube, salt walls and snip cuts are drawn smooth. F5
  • A stain where the mould has been. A faint film builds where tube has stood for about 10 s, and halves every 20 s once it has gone. F6
  • The lamp looks like light, a soft warm pool in the same shape the simulation shines, brightening what it lands on, instead of a lens with a bright ring.
  • The bottom bar stops overlapping on smaller windows, where the tool tiles now shrink.

A starving dish (22.5°), 180 s in

The review's most important finding: in the first version, tube that had lost its food stayed orange for 6 s, so you could not see the mould starving. Now it goes grey the moment it is hungry, exactly as the old drawing did. Left is the old drawing, right the new one.

With food flow on, 40 s in

The old colour was clamped full on over more than half the tube, so fed tube was flat yellow and hungry tube was a grey smear. The ramp now uses its whole range: rich tube is bright gold, and the far branch that carries almost no food goes grey.

Your pick: which grains, or none?

Each grain is one walker. They can be drawn darker than the tube or lighter. A is the default for now. The reviewer found they can read as pepper in a still picture, so a third answer is to turn them off (F4 cycles all three in the game). Which do you prefer?
