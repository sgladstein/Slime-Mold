You can see which slime your Mind rounds hold

Blind, Sour, Spin, Provoke and Still kill nothing: they change how the slime they hit behaves for a few seconds. Until now the only sign was the round's pink tracer, so you could not tell which slime was blind or still, or when it would wake. Now every walker under a status glows rose, the Mind cards' own colour, in a soft halo the size of a cell. Thick slime takes a rose wash, scattered walkers show rose cores, and the glow fades over the status's last 1.5 seconds, so you can see it running out. Each pair is one game drawn two ways: the Fork, with the squad held at 150,265 firing one kind of Mind round, 22 to 26 s, as the first statuses land on the slime coming in.

What to look for

  • Rose slime is held by a Mind round. Gold slime beside it is acting on its own.
  • The rose fading is the status running out: it starts to fade 1.5 s before the slime wakes.
  • All five statuses share the one colour, the Mind cards' family colour. What the slime does tells them apart: spinning slime curls, still slime stops, provoked slime charges, blind slime walks straight on and sour slime turns from its food.

How it is drawn

The status is the simulation's own: one word a walker, holding its kind and the step it ends. The view reads that word for every walker it draws and lays a soft disc the size of a cell, whose strength is the time left. Under thick slime the disc tints the body; on a lone walker it lights its core. Nothing is drawn where no walker holds a status.

Filmed on the software renderer, 30 frames a game second. A rose pixel has red of at least 140, blue of at least 90, and blue 15 above green; the slime's gold and orange and the blood's dark red all have blue below green, so before only the rounds' own tracers count.
