Crystals cut as gems, and spent ones broken

The polish review found the crystals read as clip-art cubes: one six-sided pyramid in three flat tones, the same on every planet. A spent one was a grey umbrella as big and as tall as a live one. Now a crystal is cut like a gem: a steep bevelled rim, a crown of twelve facets, and a darker flat table with fracture lines lit by the charge. A spent husk sits lower and darker, in seven shards, and its rim has crumbled into the ground.

What changed

  • The cut, from the edge in: a girdle bevel (first 12% of the way to the middle, slope 2.4), a crown (to 58%, slope 0.85) whose six faces each split in two and tilt 0.38 rad apart, then a flat table at 70% of the face colour.
  • A pixel-wide dark seam where facets meet, from zoom 4 up.
  • Two short fracture lines a crystal, placed by its index, lit by its charge: they fade as it drains.
  • Spent: shaded at 0.2 of full height (was 0.45), albedo 56-64 (was 66-76, about rock's value now), 7 shards (was 11), finer cracks, and the outer rim crumbles by up to 45% of the way in once under half charge; its shadow follows the crumble.
  • Charge, the take's white-hot glow, slime over it and its shadow all work as before. Cost: a crystal's own pixels only, a few dozen operations each.

Tried and dropped

  • Fracture lines right across the stone: read as scratches on its face, like the Sprawler's dashes the review also flagged.
  • Cyan light pooled on the ground round a full crystal: barely seen at zoom 6, and what showed was a blur past the crisp edge (art rule 4, and the edge test caught it).
  • A crumble from one fine noise: a spray of pixel islands at zoom 12. Now one broad bite plus a finer break.
  • A moving glint: not from the simulation, so left out.
