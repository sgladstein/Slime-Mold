Which bullet does each card change?

Six ways to show it on the gun row, from a light touch to Scott's idea of putting modifiers on a bullet instead of in its own slot. Every panel uses the same gun with the same seven cards, so you can compare them side by side.

Does it wrap like Noita? No.

Each modifier and each "next bullet" card changes the next bullet to its right, and nothing reaches back round to the start. Here is what happens to the leftovers:

• A modifier, a Delay or a Thick with no bullet after it goes onto the gun's plain bullet, which is fired as an extra shot (balance review, 11-20). That way, a lone Wide still changes something.
• A Trigger, an Expire or a Multicast with no bullet after it does nothing. Since #806, the shop holds those cards back until you own two bullets.
• "Always" cards (Pierce, Split, Seek, Scorch, Tinder and the rest) ride on every bullet, wherever they sit in the row.

Wrapping could be added as a rule: leftovers would reach the first bullet instead of the plain one. That changes what the cards do as well as how they look, so it is a separate choice from these looks. It is listed at the end.

The sample gun

The slots read: Wide, Standard blast, Trigger, Needle bullet, Gas, Pierce, Delay. In one turn this gun fires:
Shot 1 a Standard blast made wider. Shot 2 a Needle bullet that carries the Gas and lets it out where it hits. Shot 3 the gun's plain bullet, with the leftover Delay. Pierce goes on all of them. Today the row shows none of this: it is seven tiles, and "Shot 3" is not drawn anywhere.

How it would work: slots hold bullets only, and the gun's slot count becomes a bullet count. Each bullet has two sockets for a modifier, a "next bullet" card, or a Trigger, which links it to the bullet on its right. A gun strip holds the Always cards. A leftover modifier has nowhere to go, because every modifier sits on a bullet, so the plain-bullet rule and wrapping both stop mattering. Here the Delay has gone onto the gun strip, which would make it apply to every bullet. That is a different card from today's.

For: it is the clearest of the six: what a card changes is the card it sits on. Dropping a modifier onto a bullet is easy, and there are no wasted slots.
Against: it changes the game. Slots, prices and the guns' slot counts all need retuning. The Noita-like puzzle of ordering a row goes away, and Delay and Thick need new homes. Engine, Look and Direction would all share the work.

Squad and arsenal's pick

B with D on top; C as a bigger, later choice

Fused shots (B) make the grouping visible at rest and show the hidden plain bullet. They keep the row as an ordered puzzle, so no card's rules or price change. Adding the hover trace (D) explains carries and Always cards in words, and the same code can drive the in-play card bar. Both are look-only, so they can land without retuning.

Putting modifiers on a bullet (C) is clearer still, but it is a rules change. Slots become bullets, Delay and Thick need homes, and prices and guns need retuning. If Scott likes it, it should be a Milestone 2 card of its own, built after B or instead of it.

Separate choice, wrapping: (1) keep today's rule, where leftovers go on the plain bullet, or (2) wrap like Noita, where leftovers reach round to the first bullet. Option 1 makes a lone Wide always do something. Option 2 makes ordering a deeper puzzle. With C, neither applies.
