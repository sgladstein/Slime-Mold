# Mould simulation at scale: plan forward (2026-11-10)

Plan only, nothing built. Asked for by the owner on 2026-11-10 after the
Jenson review ([`jenson-physarum-2026-11-10.md`](jenson-physarum-2026-11-10.md)):
"a comprehensive plan forward, including GPU development, not just the 5

doc https://claude.ai/artifact/NhwbErNYntvbu53kh39szf (the doc has the
roadmap drawn; here it is a list). Owner's standing goal: interesting
simulations that can become a game, not realism.

## The plan in brief

Move the mould's walkers and trail onto the GPU so a dish can hold hundreds of thousands to millions of walkers, and keep the game's rules, scoring and tools on the CPU. Get there in six gated phases. The first two are cheap and tell us whether the big version is worth building before any GPU code is written.

1. **Foundations on the CPU.**
    - Add a dish hash and a determinism test.
    - Choose the number format and the scaling rule.
    - Test the two riskiest port assumptions at today's scale: walkers moving all at once, and a slower, later census.
    - Write the new rules as plain CPU code that later serves as the GPU's reference.
2. **See the scale before building it.** Render a bigger dish offline on the CPU, with the economy on, and run paired seeds. A quick GPU timing test runs on the owner's machine at the same time.
3. **GPU sandbox.** A new binary with walkers, trail, drawing and the lamp and oats on the GPU, and no game.
4. **Game rules on the GPU sim.** Food, sleep, births and deaths on the GPU. The census runs on the CPU from a fine readback and sends its labels back. One existing dish is ported end to end.
5. **Choose which dishes survive a dense network, then port and retune those.**
6. **New play that only scale allows:** contagion as a tool, growth fronts, sweeping events, territory, a large world.

**On research: the reading for the design is done; one targeted pass is still needed before writing GPU code.** The papers and Jenson's work are read in full. What has not been read is any implementation's actual source: so far only READMEs. Reading three codebases (Bleuje, Lague, PolyPhy) is the first task of phase 3, and Jones 2011 is needed before porting growth and shrinkage. Every source has a card in [`../research/sources/`](../research/sources/README.md).

## What the independent review changed

An independent reviewer checked this plan against the code and pixels' source on 2026-11-10 and found it technically sound, but three things were underestimated. The planner re-checked the main claims (`FED_EVERY`, pixels' `render_with`, the container's missing Vulkan driver) and re-ran the step benchmark (single core: 256 x 160 with 2,200 walkers 0.13 ms a step; 1024 x 640 with 100k 11.2 ms; 1024 x 640 with 1M 96.5 ms; 2048 x 1280 with 1M 110.8 ms; the reviewer's run gave 0.13, 9.5, 98 and 112). The plan below includes all of the findings.

## The GPU design

The GPU owns everything there are millions of (walkers and fine fields). The CPU owns everything there are only a few of (piles, tools, pieces, the score). The census is where the two meet every step, so its labels travel both ways: the CPU reads where tube is and sends back which walkers are fed and which piece they belong to.

**On the GPU, each step, as compute passes on the device pixels already opened:**

1. **Sense, turn, move**, one thread per walker. Each walker reads trail and scent at three sensors, turns by Jones's rule, and steps. Where a rule needs it, a parameter is set per walker or per cell: reach set by density (36 Points), stochastic steering (MCPM), and infection (contagion). The lamp keeps today's rule of pushing walkers into free cells. A steering version was tried and cut the tube it was meant to steer (the `light_weight` dead end).
2. **Claim and deposit.** When walkers compete for one cell (the crowd limit, the lamp's push, births into empty cells), a claim pass decides it: the lowest hash of walker id and step wins, through `atomicMin`. Trail deposits are u32 fixed point added atomically, so totals are the same in any order. An overflow budget covers piles of up to 80,000 food (`level.rs:310`).
3. **Diffuse and decay**, one thread per cell: a fixed-point 3x3 mean and keep factor for trail and the contagion channel, with salt and the rim as walls. Scent, light, wet and salt may stay on today's grid, depending on the scaling rule chosen in phase 1.
4. **Economy**, one thread per walker: eat, pay upkeep, sleep and starve, using the fed and piece labels the CPU sent up. Per-piece birth budgets come from a segmented prefix sum in walker-id order. Births and deaths use a fixed pool of walker slots compacted by a prefix sum, so nothing depends on thread timing.
5. **Draw**, a fragment shader straight from the fields. This ports `look.rs` (5,235 lines), and the CPU's 3.83 ms of drawing counts as saved only after a blind A/B against today's look. Hunger is drawn from the fine field, never from coarse census cells.

**On the CPU:** tools write small patches into GPU buffers (lamp position, oats, salt strokes, snip lines). The census reads back a fine bit-mask of where tube is (about 80 KB at 1024 x 640) plus food and walker counts. It runs today's BFS at full resolution on a worker thread and uploads the fed and piece labels. Phase 1 measures how late those labels can arrive, instead of assuming it.

**Fitting into pixels.** pixels 0.17 exposes its `device` and `queue`, but it skips the render closure when the window is hidden, and its scaling renderer clears the target. So simulation passes go on their own command encoder each frame, and the field shader and HUD go through a small compositor. GPU timestamps need `TIMESTAMP_QUERY`, requested through `PixelsBuilder::device_descriptor`. The simulation takes a `&wgpu::Device`, so `mouldprobe` and CI can run it without a window.

**Two grids.** Trail and walkers can be four times finer each way (1024 x 640, about 650,000 cells, one cell per pixel at today's window), while game rules keep measuring in today's cells. A finer grid alone slows the mould and shortens its smell range in dish terms, so phase 1 picks the scaling rule before phase 2's pictures.

**Determinism and tests.** (Revised by phase 1, 2026-11-10: fixed-point trail and scent changed play, so the fields stay `f32`; see *Phase 1 progress*.) Recommended at first: an all-integer simulation: fixed-point positions, headings from a shared sin/cos table, fixed-point diffusion, and random numbers from a hash of seed, walker id and step. Then the CPU reference, the GPU and software Vulkan give the same bits, seeds pair across all three, and censuses can run in cloud sessions, which have no GPU. The twin test compares exact bits. If floats are kept, it can only compare one step from an injected state, because float dishes drift apart. Software Vulkan (lavapipe) runs in the existing CI test job at small sizes.

## Phases

Each phase ends on a gate with a named measure, judged the repo's way: pictures first, then paired seeds against idle and random hands, with frame cost quoted.

**1. Foundations on the CPU**

- Add a dish hash and a determinism test, the first item in the Pixel_Physics review, so every later speed-up can be proved to change nothing.
- Add a frame profiler that splits simulation, drawing and readback.
- Add a **"GPU semantics" mode** on the CPU: walkers move all at once against the last step's trail, a claim pass settles conflicts, and deposits are integers. Run every dish's census paired against today's rules.
- Add a **delayed-census arm**: fed and piece labels 4 times a second and two frames late, instead of 20 times a second and current. Run every dish's census paired against today's rules. This sets how fresh the readback must be.
- Decide integer or float, and the scaling rule: scent, light, wet and salt kept on today's grid with only trail fine, more steps a frame, or both.
- Add the new rules to `Params`, all off by default:
    - contagion: a mark bit per walker plus a short-lived signal field;
    - a crowd limit N per cell, with N = 1 meaning today's rule;
    - sensor reach set by local trail density;
    - a stochastic steering exponent.
- **Deliverable:** the rules with tests, the two arms' census tables, and a picture page at today's scale. The crowd-limit change goes up as a blind A/B.
- **Gate:** with every flag off, the dish hash and every census table are unchanged. Each dish keeps its ship bar under both arms, or the design changes before phase 3. Contagion shows as a spread: how far its front gets past a snip across 16 seeds. Frame cost is quoted for each rule.

**2. See the scale before building it**

- Add an offline example that runs phase 1's rules, with the economy and census on, on 1024 x 640 with about 100,000 walkers (about 10 ms a step on one core), plus a few GIFs at 1,000,000. 2048 x 1280 is dropped: about 110 ms a step. A cheaper arm runs 512 x 320 with about 20,000 walkers, which multi-core CPU code might run live.
- Sweep walker count, crowd limit, angles, the density rule and the scaling rule.
- Run the lamp and oats on the same big dishes, measured by mouldprobe's `pull=1` (how far oats still pull) and by how far the lamp pushes a tube aside (5 to 7 cells today). The 2026-11-10 review found the lamp's effect gets lost in the mould's own rewiring at 22.5 degrees, and a finer network could make it worse.
- **GPU timing test on the owner's machine**, run through Remote Control: about 300 lines of wgpu that move 1,000,000 walkers, to measure the real frame cost before phase 3.
- **Deliverable:** a blind A/B page with today's dish against the scaled versions, next to Jenson's clips, plus the paired-seed tables.
- **Gate:** on paired seeds on a join dish and on Running Dry, the plan hand still beats idle and random at the bigger scale. Then the owner picks a target grid, walker count and look, or decides it isn't worth it and takes the Pixel_Physics multi-core renderer instead.

**3. GPU sandbox**

- Read the Bleuje, Lague and PolyPhy code.
- Add `--bin mouldgpu`, running the compute and draw passes on pixels' device, with the simulation on its own encoder.
- The lamp (as a push), oats and salt work as field writes. Presets come from 36 Points-style parameter sets, and contagion is on a key.
- Measure frame time with GPU timestamps. Add the twin test, with lavapipe in the existing CI job.
- **Deliverable:** a toy the owner can play with at the chosen scale, with GIFs on a page.
- **Gate:** the chosen scale runs at 60 frames a second with the GPU busy under half the frame on the owner's machine. The same seed gives the same pictures twice, and the twin test passes (exact bits, if integer).

**4. Game rules on the GPU simulation**

- Port per-walker food, upkeep, sleep and starvation: the claim pass for crowding and births, birth budgets from the segmented prefix sum, and the slot pool. Jones 2011's crowd and sparse windows come from the per-cell counts.
- The census runs on the CPU from the fine bit-mask readback and sends its labels back up.
- The rival mould is a second whole dish, with its own walkers, trail, economy and census (`hold_foe_cells`, `fight_losses`, pile keys), not a second trail channel.
- Port one dish end to end: Running Dry, which leans hardest on the food economy.
- **Gate:** on 16 paired seeds the ported dish keeps its shape. The plan hand wins, while idle and random lose, with sign-test p quoted. The frame budget still holds.

**5. Choose the dishes, then port and retune them**

- From the phase 2 pictures and phase 4, decide which dishes survive a dense network, then port those from the lesson, the three challenge dishes, Keep It Alive and The Wild One.
- Re-derive the constants measured in cells (`TUBE_WIDTH`, `OATS_PULL`, star bars) and in walkers (`table` 40, `INOCULUM` 400, `WILD_START` 600, `START_STORE`, `crowd` 7). Point `mouldprobe` at the GPU path.
- Keep the CPU path until this gate passes, then decide whether it stays as a small-dish fallback.
- **Gate:** every ported dish meets its ship bar on 16 seeds, and the owner plays them and prefers them.

**6. New play that only scale allows**, each with its own report and census:

- contagion as a tool: an alarm that pulls mould back, or a feast that lets it grow;
- growth fronts as the thing you feed, as in network_2;
- sweeping events, as in the explosion clip;
- territory between two pattern regimes, which is Bloomfront's ground;
- a large scrolling world, the forest floor from above;
- drying into a sclerotium.

**How this sits with work already queued:**

- Playing Keep It Alive and The Wild One, and tracing Keep It Alive's luck, are independent of all this; they can happen any time.
- From the Pixel_Physics review, take the dish hash and the determinism test now (phase 1). Hold the multi-core renderer port until the phase 2 gate, because GPU drawing would replace it.
- Bloomfront's Stage 0 tests design risk on today's CPU dish and can run alongside. Its two-mould step moves to the GPU in phase 4.
- The forest floor wants a large world. It is best built after phase 3 if the GPU goes ahead, or small on the CPU if not.

## Roadmap

Phases run in order and each starts only when the gate before it passes. Phase 2's gate is the one real fork: it is where the owner sees the big mould and decides whether to build it.

1. Foundations on the CPU: dish hash, GPU-style moves and a late census tested at today's scale, new rules, integer maths. Gate: flags off gives the same dish hash; dishes hold with a late census.
2. See the scale: 1024 x 640 with 100k walkers, economy, lamp and oats on, GPU timing on the owner's machine. Gate: the owner picks a target scale, or stops and stays on the CPU.
3. GPU sandbox. Gate: 60 fps with the GPU under half a frame, repeatable.
4. Game rules on the GPU: fine readback, labels sent back, Running Dry ported. Gate: plan wins, idle and random lose, on 16 paired seeds.
5. Choose and port the dishes that survive; cell and walker constants re-derived. Gate: every ship bar met, and the owner prefers it in play.
6. New play from scale. Each idea gets its own report and census.

## Risks and the owner's decisions

The biggest risk is not the GPU code. It is whether the player's hand still shows in a mould a hundred times busier, which is why phase 2 asks that question with pictures and paired seeds before anything is built.

| Risk | Why it matters | What the plan does about it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tools get lost at scale | A finer, livelier network rewires around the lamp; the 2026-11-10 review saw this at 22.5 degrees | Phase 2 measures the lamp and oats on the big dishes, with paired seeds against idle and random hands, before the gate |
| The census lags | It feeds the economy every step; labels from a slow readback arrive about 300 ms old and could change who eats and who starves | Phase 1's delayed-census arm measures it on every dish; labels go back up to the GPU |
| A finer grid changes the pace | Walkers and scent move about a cell a step, so 4x finer means slower growth and shorter smell, like the sensor-reach dead end | Phase 1 picks a scaling rule before phase 2's pictures |
| Nowhere to measure | Cloud sessions have no GPU, and float results cannot be paired across machines | An integer simulation lets censuses run on the CPU here; frame-time gates run on the owner's machine through Remote Control |
| Game rules on the GPU | Food, sleep, births and deaths per walker are the hardest part to make parallel and deterministic | A claim pass for conflicts, birth budgets from a segmented prefix sum, a slot pool; phase 4 ports one dish first |
| Results differ between machines | GPU floats can differ across vendors in the last bits | An all-integer simulation gives the same bits everywhere; if floats stay, census tables name the machine and the twin test checks one step |
| The owner's GPU is unknown | The frame budget in phase 3 depends on it | Phase 2's timing test on the owner's machine, and decision 2 below |
| Retuning | Every constant in cells moves with the finer grid, and every constant counted in walkers moves with the walker count | Phase 5 re-derives them dish by dish against the probe |
| Two code paths for a while | The CPU and GPU versions can drift apart | The twin test, and phase 5 decides whether the CPU path stays |
| Licences | Bleuje (CC BY-NC-SA) and Lague (GPL-3.0) cannot be copied into proprietary code | Read for ideas only; PolyPhy (MIT) can be reused with its notice |

**Decisions that are the owner's:**

- [ ] Start phases 1 and 2 now? Recommended: neither touches the game's behaviour with its flags off, and phase 2 answers whether the rest is worth it.
- [ ] Which machine and GPU will the owner play on? This sets phase 3's frame budget. wgpu runs on Metal (Mac), DirectX 12 or Vulkan. Phase 2's timing test runs there through Remote Control.
- [ ] Agree to an all-integer simulation? Recommended: CPU, GPU and CI then give the same bits, so results can be checked in cloud sessions without a GPU. The cost is fixed-point code throughout, and every dish's census rerun once.
- [ ] Upload Jones 2011 (*Natural Computing*, growth and shrinkage) before phase 4.
- [ ] Hold the Pixel_Physics multi-core renderer port until the phase 2 gate? Recommended, because GPU drawing would replace it.
- [ ] At the phase 2 gate: the target scale and look, or stop.
- [ ] At the phase 5 gate: does the GPU version replace the game, or sit beside it as a separate mode?

## Phase 1 progress

### 2026-11-10: where the CPU step's time goes

The frame profiler from phase 1 is built. `Dish::step_cost` times each pass
of `step_own`, and `mouldprobe` prints the split after its frame-cost run
(`players=plan seeds=1`, the free dish, scale 4, 5,880 frames, peak 1,797
walkers). Over two runs, in ms per frame (2 steps):

| Pass | ms a frame | Share of the simulation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| diffuse (trail and scent) | 0.69-0.70 | 62% |
| move (sense, turn, step, deposit) | 0.27 | 24% |
| census (one step in 6) | 0.05 | 4.5% |
| economy | 0.05 | 4.6% |
| light (lamp, water, sleep, the lamp's push) | 0.045-0.047 | 4% |
| food (piles laying scent) | 0.011 | 1% |
| flow, upkeep | 0.002 or less | 0% |
| all passes | 1.11-1.14 | of a 4.88-5.04 ms frame (drawing 3.77-3.90) |

What it changes for the plan:

- **Diffusion, not the walkers, is most of today's simulation.** The two
  3x3 passes over whole fields cost more than twice what 1,800 walkers do.
  They scale with cells, not walkers, so a 4x finer grid multiplies them by
  16 on the CPU. On the GPU they are the easiest pass, one thread per cell.
  This also backs the scaling-rule option of keeping scent on today's grid.
  Our pass is slow for its size: 0.35 ms a step, where the review's bare
  3x3 over two fields took 0.04 ms at the same grid (`step-bench.rs`). The
  difference is the per-neighbour wall test (salt and rim) and the lamp's
  fade, so the CPU pass could be sped up a lot if phase 2 needs it.
- **The census is cheap at today's size**: about 0.05 ms a frame. A slower
  census is still worth testing, because the readback is what costs on the
  GPU. Its BFS will grow with the cells too.
- **Timing changes nothing.** The timed dish is the same dish: the test
  `timing_the_step_changes_nothing` checks this, and both builds peak at
  1,797 walkers. Timed against main in the same session, the simulation took
  1.11-1.14 ms a frame timed and 1.11-1.12 untimed, inside the run-to-run
  spread.

The dish hash and the determinism test came from the Pixel_Physics review
thread's PR (#14). The arms below are built on them.

### 2026-11-10: three GPU arms on every dish

Page with the pictures and counts: https://claude.ai/artifact/VjUK3dzoMSVQSvTAPZCUaK
(copy in [`census/page.html`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/page.html)).

Three `Params` switches, all off by default, copy what the GPU design does
differently from today's CPU dish:

- **late** (`census_every` 30, `census_lag` 4): the feeding census 4 times
  a second instead of 20, its labels landing two frames after the state it
  read, as a slow readback would.
- **together** (`all_at_once`): every walker senses the trail as it stood
  before anyone moved, a walker may only enter a cell empty when the pass
  began, a claim settles two walkers after one cell (the lower key wins),
  and every random draw in the move and the lamp's push is keyed on seed,
  step and walker (`keyed`, lowbias32 in 32-bit steps only, so WGSL can run
  the same lines; the roadmap's E6 in the form the GPU needs).
- **fixed** (`fixed_point`): positions on a 1/65,536-cell grid, headings
  one of 4,096 steps with table sines and cosines on that grid, trail and
  scent rounded down onto it after each step's diffusion. This copies the
  integer design's rounding, not its speed.
- **gpu**: all three at once.

With every switch off the dish is bit for bit main's: `mouldprobe`'s
`hash=` for every hand on all five scored dishes, seed 1, matches a build of
main, and seed 2 on the three puzzles too. Tests:
`walkers_at_once_keep_one_to_a_cell`, `keyed_claims_never_tie`,
`fixed_point_keeps_the_dish_on_its_grid`.

**The census.** Every scored dish, 16 paired seeds, each arm against
today's rules ([`census/run.sh`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/run.sh), raw
output in [`census/raw/`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/raw/), tables from
[`census/analyze.py`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/analyze.py) in
[`census/tables.txt`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/tables.txt)). The hands:
idle, random, plan, fresh and the dish's intended hand on the three
puzzles; the same plus the sleeper on Keep It Alive and the warden on The
Wild One. Three arms ran on runner threads; their machines gave the same
bits as this one (the 15 seed-1 hashes, and every file both ran).

Deciding measure, named before the runs: each dish's ship bar (doing
nothing 0 stars, random at most 1, the plan hand its stars) holds as often
under the arm as today, and no hand's own measure (reach time, ruler,
mould at the bell, time lived, share eaten) moves on a paired sign test
that survives Bonferroni over the arm's 27 comparisons (p < 0.0019).

**1. A late census is safe.** Labels 4 times a second and two frames late
keep every dish's ship bar within one seed, and no hand moves on a test
that survives correction. The readback does not need to be fresh.

**2. Fixed point changes play, because floats carry a smell no one can
see.** Trail and scent fade by a factor each step and spread by a 3x3
mean, so in floats they never quite reach zero. On Behind the Wall (seed 8)
after 200 steps, 15,247 of 29,899 agar cells hold trail above zero but
below 1/65,536, and 14,021 hold such scent, with no cell's scent at zero.
Rounding to 1/65,536 sets those cells to zero. A walker whose three
sensors all read zero goes straight on (Jones's rule as written: no turn
when nothing differs), so walkers that leave the network fly in straight
lines instead of being drawn back by gradients far below anything drawn.
In the same run, floats left 0 walkers unfed from 120 steps on; fixed point
left 34-53, with 7 past the wall by step 199. Rounding only the trail left
9-14 unfed, only the scent 2-8, only positions and headings 0-1
([`census/extra/faint-smell.rs`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/extra/faint-smell.rs)).
The stills (free dish, seed 1, nobody playing, 1, 2 and 4 seconds) show
them: pale strays streak across the empty dish under fixed point
([floats](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/look-float-1-2-4s.png),
[fixed point](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/look-fixed-1-2-4s.png)). They are
why random play reaches the food behind the wall: those reaches are at 0:02
and 0:03 with no oats laid.

More bits do not fix this. In floats the faint trail and scent fade to
about 1e-38 before they vanish, which at today's keep factors is roughly
the width of the dish; no integer width a GPU handles well holds that.

**3. Moving all at once costs the warden its game on The Wild One.** Fights
where the two moulds meet fall on 13 to 15 of 16 seeds for every hand: the
median of both sides' losses goes from 2,991 to 1,884 under the warden,
5,639 to 4,560 doing nothing. The lamp's push is not the cause: with
today's one-at-a-time push restored inside the all-at-once move
([`census/extra/seqpush.diff`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/census/extra/seqpush.diff)),
the warden still did worse than today on 13 of 16 seeds (p 0.021, median
share 0.603). So it is the move itself: a walker may not step into a cell
another walker left in the same pass, and nobody senses this step's
deposits. Running Dry also gives random play more stars (10 against 3)
and loses two seeds of its ship bar.

**4. All three together** keep the warden's loss (0-16). Its puzzles pass
their ship bars only because a census every 30 steps mostly misses fixed
point's strays as they cross the food: on the five Wall seeds where random
play reached it under fixed point, a 30-step census counted one.

**What it changes in the design:**

- **Readback**: 4 times a second, two frames late, is enough.
- **Trail and scent stay `f32` on the GPU.** The all-integer recommendation
  is withdrawn for the fields: the mould depends on a smell below any
  fixed-point step. The twin test then compares one step from an injected
  state, as the float fallback above says, not exact bits over a run.
  Positions and headings could still be integers (rounding them alone
  changed little in the trace), but no census has tested that alone.
  Whether an invisible far smell should steer the mould at all is a design
  question for the new games; it is invisible, which the owner's rule on
  showing the simulation argues against.
- **Moving all at once is how a GPU moves walkers, so it is the engine,
  not a bug to tune away.** It fights less where two moulds meet, which
  matters for an RTS. The knob, if more fighting is wanted, is a second
  claim round (walkers that lost a claim or faced a cell vacated this pass
  try again after the winners move), one more GPU pass.

### 2026-11-10: the scaling rule, and phase 2's big dish on the CPU

Page with the videos, stills and counts:
https://claude.ai/artifact/WMfUwTj9Dfdwp9MCfy8MHs (copy in

**The engine alone, at any size.** [`src/mould/big.rs`](../src/mould/big.rs)
is a mould dish of any grid size with the rules phase 1 chose for a GPU:
walkers move all at once with claims (`all_at_once`'s move), keyed draws,
`f32` trail and scent. It leaves out the economy, the census, tools and
levels on purpose: the owner's 20:55Z ruling moved the gates to engine
measures on a neutral dish, and the walker count is fixed as in Jones 2010
and Jenson's sketches. The dish is an empty ellipse with seven food piles
(home first; the same layout at every scale for a seed). Field passes run
on threads by row bands and give the same bits as one thread
(`threads_change_nothing`). Its measures (`BigMeasure`), in today's cells
and seconds so scales compare: tube area (trail over 1), loops (holes of
at least one dish cell), mean tube width (twice the area over the edge),
reach from home, piles reached, and strays (walkers where the trail is
under 1). [`examples/bigdish.rs`](../examples/bigdish.rs) runs it and
writes stills, GIFs and video frames.

**The scaling rule census.** 1024 x 640 (4x finer each way) with 32,000
walkers (today's crowding), against today's 256 x 160 with 2,000, on a
ladder of arms that each add one fix to the one before. Two starts: a
blob packed on home (the game's start) and walkers spread over the dish
(Jones's start). 16 paired seeds, 20 dish seconds
([`scale/run.sh`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/scale/run.sh); seeds 9-16 ran on two
runner threads, whose today and turn hashes this machine's binary
matched on the seeds it checked; raw output in
[`scale/raw/`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/scale/raw/), tables from
[`scale/analyze.py`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/scale/analyze.py) in
[`scale/tables.txt`](gpu-plan-2026-11-10/scale/tables.txt)).

Deciding measures, named before the runs: pace from the blob (piles
reached at 20 s, reach at 5 s), shape from the spread start (loops and
tube width at 20 s), strays at 20 s from both, and the cost a tick. Each
arm paired with today on the same seed, sign test, Bonferroni over the 36
comparisons (p < 0.0014, marked *).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Medians over 16 seeds; width in dish cells. Every arm reaches all 7
piles within 20 s from the spread start. `nose` was added after the first
five arms' results; against `naive` on the same seeds (its own correction
over 6 tests) it has more loops (16 of 16), fewer strays from both starts
(16 of 16 each), and no difference in piles (5 up, 1 down) or reach at 5 s.

1. **Copying today exactly gives no network.** With every number in
   today's units (`turn`), about 16 walkers share each of today's cells and
   the trail is smooth, and the dish settles into wide tubes with no loops
   on all 16 seeds. Today's network comes partly from its coarse grain:
   one walker to a cell is noisy, and the noise seeds the loops.
2. **More steps a frame is rejected.** Four fine steps a tick (`steps`)
   costs 3.7 times the plain port and gives fewer loops and twice the
   strays, with no faster spread. A walker that turns up to 45 degrees
   every fine step turns four times as often per cell of distance.
3. **The sensor offset sets the mesh; smelling food far is free.** The
   plain port's fine mesh comes from sensing trail 9 fine cells out. Moving
   the trail sensors out to today's reach (`reach`) keeps today's pace but
   coarsens the mesh and leaves half the walkers off the network. Smelling
   only scent at today's reach (`nose`) keeps the fine mesh, cuts strays,
   and costs nothing.
4. **A packed blob stays a bag on the fine grid.** Every fine arm's home
   blob holds together as a bag with a bright rim and sends out a few thin
   tubes, where today's breaks into arms. The fine blob is about 24 sensor
   reaches across (106 fine cells in radius against a 9-cell reach) where
   today's is 6; `reach`, whose blob is 6 reaches across, kept today's pace.
   The game grows its mould from a small inoculum by births, which this
   dish leaves out, so the bag may not arise there; it is checked when the
   economy is ported (phase 4).

**The GPU timing test, built 2026-11-10.** [`examples/gputime.rs`](../examples/gputime.rs)
runs the big dish's step under the chosen rule as four wgpu compute passes
a tick (on the `wgpu` that `pixels` already brings, so no new
dependency): `sense` (a thread a walker: sensors, turn, aim, `atomicMin`
claim on a cell empty when the pass began), `walk` (the winner moves and
counts a deposit with `atomicAdd`; losers draw a new heading), `trail`
and `scent` (a thread a cell: 3x3 mean over agar, kept; `scent` also
clears the counts and claims and advances the tick). Random draws use the
CPU's `keyed` stream, so the GPU dish can be checked against the CPU's. It
times rounds of 120 ticks a submit, reads per-pass GPU time from timestamp
queries when the adapter has them, reads the dish back and prints the
engine's measures beside the CPU dish's for the same seed, and can write
a still.

Checked in the cloud container on a software Vulkan driver (llvmpipe,

measures at 10 s, seed 1, spread start. These timings are a CPU emulating
a GPU, not GPU numbers.
