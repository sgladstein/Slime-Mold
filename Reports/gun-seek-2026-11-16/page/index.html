Seeking bullets

You picked homing in the shot trace and asked for hit % beside slime killed. Built in both engines from the live slime: a seeking bullet that reaches the point its soldier aimed at with nothing on its line strikes the slime beside it there. On thin slime, seekers hit 89% of shots against 80% with plain bullets (higher in 13 of 13 paired fights; misses 88 down to 47) and kill 554 against 538. On a thick front nothing changes. The rescued hits land on thin edges, so kills rise far less than hits.

Every clip loops. Slow motion shows the bullets bending.

How it works, and what didn't

  • A soldier's bullet flies on to its full range. A miss is a bullet that passed beside the walkers it was aimed at, their trail too faint to stop it, and flew on.
  • Where it reaches its target, a seeker whose line holds nothing looks 2 cells either side (a card) and strikes the first slime or walker there. Both engines do it from the slime as it is that frame, so the GPU matches the CPU exactly.
  • Catching beside the line everywhere (my first build) hit 93% of shots but struck the thin fringe instead of the body: on the thick front kills fell from 471 to 266.
  • Catching only at the end of the range, or stopping on walkers on the line, changed nothing: the end of the range is far past the target, and misses pass beside their walkers, not through them.

Why grid homing couldn't fix the misses

  • The game steers by a walker count, a second old. A bullet stops only on slime trail thick enough to show. Against thin slime, misses are bullets that cross walkers whose trail is too faint to stop them, or walkers that have already moved. A seeker steered by that count chases the same walkers.
  • The ceiling is low. Only one shot in five misses against thin slime, one in ten on a front. Even a homing card that saved every miss would add at most a fifth more hits where the slime is thin.
  • "Bend only bullets with nothing on their line", which I told you I'd build, failed for the same reason: the count can't say what a bullet will strike. Every seeker going for the nearest slime keeps your condition (it never skips the walkers in front) without guessing.

Your call then (you chose homing in the shot trace)

  • Drop homing and go on to step D, bullet families, Mind first (recommended). The miss counter and these findings stay.
  • Keep Seek as a small card. Bullets visibly bend; about 6% more kills on thin slime for 2 of a gun's 10 budget.
  • Build homing into the shot trace. Both engines would bend a seeking bullet cell by cell toward the thickest trail it passes, using the live slime instead of a second-old count. A few hours of work, and still under that ceiling.

Why it changes so little

  • A shot stops at the first slime it meets (your rule from 11-11). The front is a solid wall of slime, so a bullet that bends still hits the slime in front of it, a cell or two from where it would have.
  • Soldiers already aim at the thickest slime in sight, and their hits were already spread: in the plain game to 39 s, 215 hits fell in 214 different cells. There is no crowding for seek to fix.
  • Bending toward a mini-boss does not help when the slime round it is in the way. The plan warned that plain homing "lands where the bullet was going anyway", and this measured it.

Prototype: seekers fly over the slime to a mark

Built on the branch to try the first answer below. Once a seeker has flown its first 3 cells (so slime on the squad is still shot), it picks a mark ahead: a mini-boss, else the slime feeding on a crystal. It then flies over the slime between and strikes only when it gets there. The clips start at 34 s, when the mini-boss starts its hunt.

Here, finally, seeking does something: buckshot pellets reach the mini-boss instead of spending themselves on the slime in front, and the hits bunch onto it (40 cells against 75). The cost is the slime in between, which is left alone. With plain bullets the two came out about even on the mini-boss. In the first version the seekers flew over from the muzzle, and that squad lost 4 of 7 soldiers in 8 s because it stopped shooting the slime on itself. Crystal feeders seldom became marks in these scenes, because a squad on its own only fires at slime near it.
