Larder World · the run · round 1, 2026-11-16

Mini-boss slimes

Your idea, widened: stronger enemies that form out of the slime during a planet. This is round 1: outside research on how other games do elites and bosses, and three independent designers (encounters, simulation, looks and wildcards) working from a neutral brief, about 90 ideas between them. It ends with questions for you. Nothing is built yet; every look below will come as a clip once you pick.

What a sub-unit looks like today

A raid, and a break-away piece, leave the body as a stream of single specks. Each walker climbs the same scent on its own and nothing makes the front wait for the back, so nothing on screen reads as one thing that is stronger than the rest.

All three designers put the same experiment first: a held body. The two cohesion rules tried before (only step next to others; keep heading and lay trail when blocked) failed because they were switched on for the whole slime. Given only to a separate sub-population with its own trail, "tight crawling lumps" and "still droplets" are exactly what a mini-boss should look like. What was missing is a push that differs front to back, and the body can make that itself by avoiding the ground it just covered.

First experiment: a held body

The piece every option needs, tried on the CPU engine while you read: 600 walkers on an empty dish, sent to a crystal 70 cells away. Not yet in the game or on the GPU.

Two small rules did it. A steady pull toward the target is added to the trail and scent each walker smells, where today's break-aways and raids replace what they smell with the target and stream. A leash: a walker that strays more than 1.3 times the body's radius from its centre runs back toward it, so the front waits for the back. The one-body rules tried before (only step next to others, slow down when alone) did not stop the stream with the pull on. The leash needs the body's centre, one sum a step over the body's own walkers, so its cost is the body's size, not the map's. Next: give it to a piece of the real slime in the game, on the GPU, and film the forming.

Where the three designers agree

Keep the bronze, fix the break-away. Condensed walkers stay as the material (armour, double bite) and the first stage of forming. The break-away's speck stream is what becomes the first mini-boss.
A chance on a cause you can see. Not a die every minute: the odds rise with crowding, long feeding, a part cut off from food, or your buildings nearby. Capped per planet.
Forming is the warning, and the counterplay. 20 to 40 s of veins turning toward one spot, a bright spot on the minimap, a sound. Shoot it while it forms and it hatches smaller. The research found the same in StarCraft cocoons, Deep Rock Galactic's veined cocoons and slimes fusing into a king.
Kinds from parts, not a bestiary. A body (how it holds and moves), an errand (what it goes for) and a temper (the planet's tier). About five bodies give dozens of kinds, and the planet's tier picks the family, so each planet's champion teaches that tier.
It should go for what you build, not only your soldiers: pumps, walls, turrets, fresh crystals.
Kill it and it pays. Its stored food is left as a puddle a squad can drain for shop money. Ignore it and it does its errand: steals income, takes a crystal, opens a wall.
Soldiers' aim decides half of this. Squads shoot what is nearest, so a mini-boss inside ordinary slime is shielded for free. Squads need to prefer it when it is in range.
A boss, yes, but the slime itself. No separate monster with a health bar: the slime's remnant becomes the boss (below).

A director caps the count: one alive at a time on planets 1 to 3, two after, none in the first minutes, a few per planet early and more late (planets run 15 to 55 minutes). The research's best models: Left 4 Dead deals its boss events like a deck with "nothing" in it, Space Marine 2 allows 0 to 2 a level only after a cooldown, and Risk of Rain 2 prices elites in its budget (6 times a normal enemy, 36 for the top tier).

The boss question

The research found full bosses go wrong in roguelikes when a one-enemy arena replaces the systems the player learned. All three designers said yes to a boss, but as the slime itself, not a separate monster:

  • Last stand. Near the end of a planet, the slime's remnant gathers from across the map into one held body and fights or runs. Pieces you already cut off wither on the way, so its size is set by how you played. It turns the mop-up hunt for stragglers into a chase with a climax. The defenses thread already built a last stand as a rush of every walker (off, since you were unsure); this would make it a body.
  • A boss planet. The core world's slime as one organism: a heart on the biggest crystal cluster, with organs (a Mortar, a Fist, a Leech) fed through cords. Cut a cord and that organ withers. At half mass the heart buds and moves to another cluster.

Questions for you

  1. Which to prototype first? Pick up to three from the options, or name others. My pick: the Hunter (your idea), the Fist on a stalk (it hits what you build and has a cut-or-shoot choice), and the Mortar (it makes you push in). All three need the held body, which I am starting on now.
  2. Condensation: replace it, or build on it? My pick: keep the bronze dense walkers as the stuff mini-bosses are made of and as the first stage of forming, and replace the break-away's stream of specks with the Wanderer.
  3. Should every mini-boss come to you, or should some stay put so you have to go to them? My pick: a mix. If every threat walks to you, you never choose to push.
  4. Pure random chance, or a chance that rises with a cause you can see? (crowding, long feeding, a part cut off, your buildings nearby), capped per planet, with a forming you can shoot to make it smaller. My pick: the cause you can see.
  5. Boss: a last stand, a boss planet, both, or neither for now? My pick: build the last stand as an option and film it; hold the boss planet until the mini-bosses play well.
  6. Should squads shoot a mini-boss first when it is in range? The gun review is asking about targeting cards too, and I would line this up with that thread. My pick: yes, by default, without a card.
