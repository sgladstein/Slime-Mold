Step C · run length and route choice · 11-25

Five ways to choose a route

Today a run is a fixed line of eight planets. The slime species is set by depth (Drifter, then Sprawler, Trunk, Swarmer, Sporer, Sleeper, Grudge, Dozer), with a shop between planets. Twists and the rescue goal turn up by chance. Planets take 4 to 9 minutes, so a run is about an hour. Each direction below would add choice and reach about two hours. They can be mixed. Costs are my estimates in thread-days.

The pieces already exist: generated planets for any seed and depth, eight biomes, eight slime species, two twists, the rescue goal, starfall rewards, save and resume, and the Past runs page.

1. A star map with forks

The classic roguelike map: 12 to 14 steps, two or three planets to pick from at each step, all converging on a home world. Each planet shows its slime, biome, twist, goal (the heart, a rescue, something new) and reward before you land. You plan a route around your guns. If your build has no fire, you steer away from the Trunk.

Length picks the number of stepsReplay highCost about 2 days: a map screen, the run's graph, save format

2. The slime spreads between planets

The star map itself runs a slime simulation at a large scale. Between your landings the mould sends tubes along the shortest routes between planets, the network-finding real Physarum is known for. Planets on a thick tube get bigger and better fed. Freeing a planet cuts its tubes, so whatever you skip keeps growing. The run ends at the home world, which is as strong as the network still feeding it. Your route becomes a cut through a living network. This is the most "the mould is the game" option.

Length as many planets as you choose to freeReplay very highCost about 3 to 4 days, on top of direction 1's map

3. Draft the slime you face

There is no map. At each depth you pick one of two slimes, from a pool for that band of the run. Each comes with its landing card, its counter and its reward. A run becomes a draft against your own build: take the Sporer now while you have Germ, or face the Swarmer for double points. It's the smallest change and it teaches the counters.

Length 12 picksReplay mediumCost about 1 day: a pick screen and species pools

4. Contracts and heat on each planet

Each planet offers optional contracts for extra points or a rare card: rescue the dug-in squad, take a crystal without losing one, break the mini-boss. You can also turn on heat (a twist, a bigger heart, faster raids) for a bigger reward. You choose how hard each planet is. This adds length and decisions without new planets, and it fits inside any of the other directions.

Length longer planets by choiceReplay mediumCost about 1 day

5. Planets remember you

When you leave a planet after reaching a goal short of a full clear (a rescue, a drained heart crystal), it stays on the map with its slime regrowing from what you left. Later you can go back for a bigger reward, or it sends raids to the home world. This uses save and resume to carry a planet's state forward. A planet becomes a place with a history, not just a level.

Length revisits add planetsReplay highCost about 2 to 3 days. Needs direction 1's map

My recommendation

Build direction 1's star map as the frame, with direction 4's contracts as what each planet offers, at about 12 steps. That gives a two-hour run you choose, in about 3 days. Then grow direction 2, the spreading slime, on the same map. It's the most original idea and the one where the mould is the game at the run's scale too, but it rests on the map existing first. Direction 3 is the fallback if you want something quick.
