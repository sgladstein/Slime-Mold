# Heartwood playtest (2026-11-08)

## 1. The open question and my answer

**Does choosing which tip gets which card feel like a decision, and can a player say before playing what the tree will look like after?**

**A decision: no, not yet.**
- Strategies that choose nothing land within about 5 points of the builder's plan:
  - pressing 1 every half second on whatever tip the game has already picked: 37.8%;
  - playing only Leaf Out: 37.2%;
  - the builder's plan: 42.0%.
- The plan beats the key-masher on only 7 seeds of 12.
- The strategy that wins most chooses least. Shift-select every tip and play card 1 onto all of them: **51.8% mean, 15 stars, beats the plan 9-3**, with only 19 cards played.
- So the real decision today is "put each card on every tip", and that makes Fork, the designed multicast, redundant.

**Predicting the tree: partly.**
- One card is predictable. The tip ends a median 3 cells from the dotted outline, for every strategy (it reproduces the builder's number).
- The tree is not. A card is 3 to 16 cells on a crown of over 1,000. The outline is a faint dotted line a few pixels long (picture 2).
- A quarter to a third of cards (plan: 26 of 82) are called off after they are paid for ("no room", "too tall for water"), and nothing warned of it beforehand.

## 4. What feels good / what feels dead

**Good**
- Doing nothing loses completely: 0% on 12 of 12 seeds.
- The grown tree looks like a real tree, thickening its trunk under its leaves (pictures 1 and 4).
- The words on screen are plain: "16 CELLS STRAIGHT UP", "THIS TIP IS WAITING FOR A CARD", the light bar.

**Dead**
- **A card's answer is small.** 3 to 16 cells, spread over about 2 s, on a tree of 1,000+. Over 80 cards a year, no single play reads.
- **Order and placement barely matter.** Thoughtless rules come within 5 points of the plan. The seed matters more than the player: the same plan scores 6% on seed 3 and 60% on seed 2, because the rival ranges from 211 to 2,900 leaves.
- **"Shade the rival out" is not happening.** Light falls straight down, the two crowns stand side by side, and the overhang race stalls at the water-height limit. The share is really "who has more leaves".
- **Sugar is not a resource after season 1.** The purse sits in the hundreds.
- **Cards fail silently.** A quarter to a third are refunded after the fact (plan: 26 of 82 a year), with no warning on the outline.

## 5. Improvements (ranked)

1. **(Do first) One card, one tip; Fork is the only multicast.** Drop shift-click play, or make it spend one hand card per tip. Today select-all beats the plan 9-3, which makes the showpiece card pointless. This is a small change in `Heartwood::play`.
2. **Make sugar bind all year.** Make card prices scale with tree size, or charge upkeep from the purse. Then each play is a trade, and choosing which tip earns its keep matters. Today the plan ends the year with 237 unspent.
3. **Fewer, bigger cards.** For example, about 20 plays a year, each of 30 to 60 cells: a whole limb, a full leaf spray. Each play then visibly changes the silhouette within a second, and a player can author a shape.
4. **A low, slanting sun.** Shadows that fall sideways onto the neighbour turn Lean and where-to-leaf into real choices, and make "shade the rival" literally true. Show the rival's shaded leaves going dark as the visible answer.
5. **An honest outline.** Draw the card's whole footprint, with the leaves for Leaf Out and both halves for Fork, bold enough to see on a phone. Turn it red with the reason ("TOO TALL FOR WATER") before the card is paid for.
6. **The same rival every game.** Start the rival from a fixed grown state, so a valley's score is about the player and not the seed.

## 6. Promise score: 2 / 5

The tree is lovely, but today the cards are a slow way of drawing it: thoughtless play nearly matches the plan, and the best strategy is to not choose at all. Fixes 1 to 3 are cheap and would tell quickly whether a real decision is in there.
