# Review 2: what the engine can really deliver, and at what cost (2026-11-07)

*Reviewer 2 of 3. My lens on top of the full job: fun weighed against the true build cost, and against the risk that the simulation does not look right in motion. Frame cost is a hard limit in this engine, and the 512x320 test world is meant to grow. I checked three claims in the source; they are listed in section 5.*

## 1. Top 5

1. **Storm Glass**: Arc, plus Fulgurite's glass, plus Explorer D's copper that stays between storms. Lightning that leaves a mark.
2. **Mould**, built a cheaper way than proposed: swarm-trail slime on the engine's existing fine-grained trail field, using tools instead of a wand.
3. **Quench**, plus Floodgate's law that boiled water comes back as rain somewhere else. The cheapest honest test of "the world multiplies the play".
4. **Firedamp**, rebuilt so that it does not depend on heat moving the air. That is not true today.
5. **Fault**: crack the ice wall. The crack tips are new, but the breakage underneath is shipped.

## 2. Combinations

**A. Arc + Fulgurite + D's copper farm = Storm Glass. Strong merge.**
- Arc's weak spot is that a bolt is gone in half a second. Its only lasting trace is an ion haze that fades in about 3 seconds.
- Fulgurite's rule fixes that: sand the bolt passes through becomes a glass tube.
- D's rule fixes it further: copper you grow stays for the whole run.
- So every strike leaves **real cells**: glass where it crossed sand, a dry scorch where it boiled a puddle, fused copper where it ran hot. Later bolts prefer glass and copper. Over a level you see your own wiring tree build up from past strikes. That is the growth network the owner asked for, made from the verb itself.
- What moves where: Fulgurite's Pull the Plug becomes an end-of-storm reveal on some levels, not a core card. Arc keeps its storm clock and its magazine to protect.
- Why it is not muddy: it is still one verb (the bolt) and one threat (the overflow strike). The merge only adds memory.

**B. Firedamp's burn band + Arc's bolt = the marsh storm. Good as a later level, muddy as a first game.**
- Lightning ignites gas only where the gas is in its burnable band. You thin a pocket with draughts so the storm lights it, and you keep the village's gas too thick or too thin.
- Lovely on paper. In the hand it asks a new player to read two invisible fields at once: where charge wants to go, and which gas will burn.
- Keep it as one "boss" level of whichever game ships first. Never make it the opening.

**C. Quench + Floodgate + Landslide = one valley game.**
- The three share a frame: something flows downhill toward a town, and you route it.
- Quench brings the best verb: aim the roof, not the water.
- Floodgate brings the best lesson: boil the lake and it rains on the town a minute later.
- Landslide brings the blast that drops a cliff into the river.
- Honest verdict: it stays coherent only if each level has **one** threat, lava or flood, never both at once. Three verbs on one valley across a run is fine. Three in one level is mud.

**D. Mould absorbs Veins (Explorer B's own proposal) and Etch's rust. Good, but later.**
- Veins' rival network and Etch's spreading rust both make good second-act opponents: a rival mould, or a creeping rust that eats food.
- Neither should be in the first prototype. Mould's question is whether gardening one organism is fun. A rival makes it a race, and a race hides the answer.

**E. Lode's multiplying score on Mould dishes. Muddier.** A mould that does what it likes, under a score that multiplies, turns small growth changes into huge unexplained swings. Keep Mould's three plain stars.

## 3. Why each would be fun, and the biggest reason it might not be

**1. Storm Glass.**
- **Why it's fun:** every press gets a loud, branching answer inside a second: a flash, a fork, a puddle bursting into steam, a lamp lighting.
- Doing nothing loses, because the cloud overflows onto the tallest thing, and the level makes that the thing you protect.
- Random play loses, because an unshaped bolt goes where the overflow would.
- The glass and copper mean the run leaves a picture behind, and you can point at it and say "I made that".
- It also suits the engine: a bolt is a few hundred cells changed once, not a body of water kept awake. The renderer already does a full-screen redraw on a strike frame and the frame after it, so the cost is already budgeted for.
- **Biggest risk:** aim. If the bolt cannot be steered on purpose, the player is rolling dice. All four explorers named this risk, and nobody has measured it.

**2. Mould.**
- **Why it's fun:** it is the most beautiful thing on this list in motion. A living veil pulses, finds food and pulls back its dead ends, and you watch it get smarter.
- The three stars (reach, thrift, toughness) are graded by nature. The end-of-dish storm cuts three tubes, which makes the network's quality visible in one beat.
- It is the owner's own follow-up interest.
- **Biggest risk:** it becomes watching. The organism decides, and you wait. The 4-second test from Explorer B (from dropping Oats to the front visibly bending) is the right gate, and I would make it 3 seconds.

**3. Quench.**
- **Why it's fun:** "aim the roof, not the water" is a real aha, and the engine already does it: steam under a roof drips back where it formed.
- It is the shortest path to finding out whether a combo plus the engine's chemistry is fun, and it costs about 2 days.
- D's forge version (the forge needs *some* lava) makes "block everything" a loss as well, which is a better challenge than C's dam outline.
- **Biggest risk:** the quench loop happens under a rock and reads as nothing happening. It is also slow, and it sits close to Creeper World and From Dust.

**4. Firedamp.**
- **Why it's fun:** dread you can see, then a release you aimed. "Too thick to burn, so thin it first" is a rule learned from one fizzle.
- **Biggest risk:** it costs more than the explorers think. The gas, the draughts that push it and the burn band are all new. The air-grid shortcut does not work (section 5), and A's most surprising combo, the fire that "breathes" its way into a pocket, depends on a coupling the engine does not have.

**5. Fault.**
- **Why it's fun:** Meltwater then Frost, where water you forgot about turns twelve hairline cracks into three falling slabs, is the best "the world was the multicast" moment of the 21 concepts.
- **Biggest risk:** breakage. Structural collapse is the subsystem that has overturned three models in this project, and a puzzle needs breakage that can be predicted. Expect to spend most of the time on whether the same cut breaks the same way.

**Just missing:** High Tide (the most instantly understood of all 21, but it is falling sand, which the owner ruled out, and the tide keeps every beach chunk awake); Lode (cheap, but the sim is decoration); Etch (the patterns are famously unpredictable, the opposite of a puzzle).

## 4. Extensions

### Storm Glass

**The verb: keep a small wand for the bolts, but shape the ground by direct drawing.**
- Rain, copper dust and glass panes are placements. Drag them with a finger, from a stock that refills each storm.
- The wand (3 to 4 slots) holds only the bolt cards: Bolt, Straight, Bushy, Heavy, Again, and the trigger On Strike. Order really matters there: Bushy first, then Again.
- Why split it: placement in a card slot is a slow way to draw a line, and the satchel showed that cards which only "set up" feel inert. Every press of the wand is then a strike.

**New verbs and surprises.**
- **Hold.** Keep the bolt button down and the leader creeps down slowly, visibly feeling its way. Release to commit, or drag sideways to nudge it toward one cell. This is the answer to the aim problem: let the player steer for half a second and keep the randomness for the forks.
- **Glass remembers.** A tube of fused sand that later floods becomes a water pipe, and Fulgurite's reveal comes free. A tube that a later bolt crosses re-fuses thicker.
- **Overload.** Thin copper that carries a heavy bolt melts and spits sparks, and the sparks start fires. Greed is punished in a way you can see.
- **Strike knocks things.** The source has a hit impulse for rigid bodies (`rigid::strike`, I saw its signature only). A Heavy bolt could knock a loose block off a ledge. That is the "verb delivers an impulse" law for free.

**The first 60 seconds.**
1. A dark sky, one charge bar, the bell tower on the left, and one lamp in the open on the right.
2. The hand is just Bolt. You press it, and the bolt goes into the bell tower, which catches fire. The game has just shown you the default path and why it is bad.
3. Rain is unlocked. You drag a wet strip toward the lamp, press Bolt, and the bolt follows the strip and lights the lamp.
4. A second lamp sits under an overhang. Then the storm overflows.

**How a session is shaped.**
- One storm is one level: five cloud-fills, about 2 minutes.
- Between storms: pick one of three bolt cards, and keep every piece of glass and copper. The map widens every three storms, as in D's version.
- The run ends when the magazine takes its third hit.

**On a phone.** Lightning is the best phone visual on the list: bright on dark, legible at any size. Lamps and the magazine need to be at least 8 cells wide to read at that size. Draw the "likeliest path" ghost as a thin dotted line, not a tint.

**The cheapest prototype that honestly asks "is this fun" (3 days).**
- **Skip the charge field entirely.** A bolt is a growing tip. Each step it weighs its neighbours by:
  - a downward pull;
  - how well the neighbour conducts (water, wet ground, copper and glass high; dry rock low; ice zero);
  - a pull toward the nearest conductor, from a distance map built once per cast;
  - the ion field.
  One weight raised to a "straightness" power gives you both bushy and spear-shaped bolts.
- Ions go on the existing fine-grained trail field. It can be written and read per cell today. Drift with the wind is new but small: shift the field by one cell every few frames.
- Effects on a hit: set fire, water to steam, sand to glass. Glass can begin as any existing solid.
- Three cards on a strip: Bolt, Straight/Bushy, Again. One lamp in the open and one under an overhang.
- **The measurement:** the same marked target over 10 seeds, hits counted. Then give one new player five tries at a lamp they choose themselves. Put a GIF page with the counts beside each picture (hits, misses onto the tower, cells fused) and the `ascii` worst frame during a cast.
- Build the real charge solve only if this bolt fails the eye test.

### Mould

**Build it on the engine's existing swarm-trail model, not on a tube graph.**
- B proposes tubes that thicken with flow, solved on a graph of junctions every few frames. The hard part is not the flow solve. It is pulling a clean graph of junctions out of a blob of cells every few frames, and that is new, fiddly code.
- The engine already holds the other published slime-mould model: many tiny agents, each laying trail and turning toward the strongest trail ahead of it (the Jones model). The fine trail field's own source comments say it was sized against a Jones follower.
- That model makes thick tubes, pruned dead ends and shortest-path networks with no graph at all. It also looks alive in motion, which is the bar.
- Mapping the tools onto it:
  - food is a steady deposit of trail;
  - Lamp is a per-cell fade, plus agents avoiding light;
  - Salt is a line agents cannot step onto;
  - Snip kills the agents in a stroke and clears the trail there;
  - the food reserve is the number of agents, so upkeep is agents dying off far from food.

**The verb: tools, not a wand.**
- The surprise in Mould comes from the organism. A wand's order grammar would add a second layer of unpredictability on top of an already unpredictable one, and the player could not tell which of the two surprised them.
- Give a small, plain stock per dish instead: 5 Oats, 2 Lamps, 1 Salt line, 3 Snips. Placing on the pulse beat gives a small bonus, which keeps the rhythm without forcing it.
- Keep "Buried" (fires when the front reaches it) as the one trigger. It is the only card-like idea that adds a real plan.

**New surprises.**
- **Memory you can see.** The crust the mould leaves is drawn faintly, so a player who Washes a region watches the mould come back to explore it.
- **Starve-to-jump** is B's best idea: a starved branch fruits, and its spores blow over the salt wall. Keep it, and make it the dish-3 lesson.
- **Two dishes touching.** A late dish places your mould and a rival's together. Where they meet they either fuse (same strain) or wall off (rival).

**The first 60 seconds.** One blob, one oat pile in plain sight, and the hand is just Oats. Drop a second pile, and within 3 seconds the front visibly leans toward it. Then Lamp. The first dish is won on reach alone.

**On a phone.** Draw tube brightness on a fixed dark-to-bright ramp, never blended with the dish colour, so thin tubes and thick tubes read apart at small size. The pulse is a travelling brightness wave and reads well on a phone.

**The cheapest prototype (3 to 4 days).**
1. Day 1: 20,000 to 30,000 agents on one trail channel over a 512x320 dish, two food sources, rendered on a fixed ramp. Look at it before anything else.
2. Day 2: Oats, Lamp and Snip as direct taps.
3. Day 3: the reserve, the three-cut storm and the three stars.
4. **The measurements:** the time from Oats to a visible bend; the paired blind-versus-planned stars over 5 seeds; and the `ascii` worst frame with the whole dish awake.

**The cost to name up front.** A living dish never settles, so the renderer's skip for unchanged areas never applies, and the whole world is redrawn every frame. A dish is a bounded world, though, so Mould is the one leading idea that never needs the world to grow. That is a point in its favour on cost.

## 5. Wrong or optimistic claims

**Checked in the source (my three):**

1. **"Heat moves the air grid" (A's Firedamp combo step 4, the Warm chimney card, and Tincture's Warm): false today.**
   - Checked in `src/sim/field.rs`, the air-velocity update. Air speed changes only with pressure differences.
   - Pressure changes only from air converging or spreading, and from pressure pushes that explosions add (`add_pressure_impulse`).
   - Temperature is *carried* by the air but never pushes it.
   - So fire does not draw a draught, Warm makes no chimney, and A's "fire breathes its way into the pocket" does not happen unless someone builds that coupling.
   - Building it is plausible, but it changes the whole world's air, which is a cost and a risk that A did not price.
   - A related problem I saw on the way: the air grid works in blocks of 16x16 cells (`FIELD_SCALE = 16`). A mine gallery 8 cells tall is half of one air block, and a 3-cell rock wall cannot block air on that grid. Firedamp's draughts should push the gas field directly, not go through the air grid.

2. **"`weather::strike` is drawing only and touches no cells" (B and D): correct.**
   - Checked in `src/sim/weather.rs`. It is a pure function of the seed and the frame, and it returns only an x position and a flash brightness. It has no access to the world.
   - Its doc comment adds that the renderer forces a full-screen redraw on every strike frame and the one after. Brief bolts are fine; a level with constant strikes is not free.
   - So Arc's lightning is new code from end to end, and only the flash drawing is reusable.

3. **"Spreading fields that can be written and read per cell" (the brief and several concepts): true for one field, false for the others.**
   - Checked in `src/sim/pheromone.rs`: world-sized planes at single-cell resolution, with per-cell `deposit`, `sample` and `scale`. They have two general channels plus an alarm channel.
   - A non-ant game can repurpose those channels for ions, gas, food scent or salt. Their own comment says to resist adding a third without a concrete use.
   - They do **not** drift with wind; drift would be new.
   - **Light and moisture are not per cell.** They live on the 16x16 air grid in `field.rs`. So:
     - A's "the moisture field stands in for wet ground conducts" is a 16-cell blur: a soaked strip 3 cells wide does not exist in it;
     - Fulgurite's "bolt prefers wet cells (moisture is an existing per-cell field)" is wrong;
     - Mould's "spreads fast on damp ground" can only be coarse;
     - wet ground for any lightning game should be a per-cell trail channel or a material.

**Unchecked, but flagged from reasoning or from text I passed on the way:**

4. **"The weather wind field" (B's Arc combo, Mould's spores).** The weather module's own comment says wind is **one number for the whole world**, shaded only by how sheltered a spot is. B's best combo, haze curling *under* an overhang to a hidden lamp, needs air that varies from place to place. One global breeze only slides the haze sideways. The haze could reach the lamp by spreading, but "the wind did the bending" is optimistic.

5. **"The charge field is solved only at cast, so it costs nothing" (A and B).** Real branching lightning models solve the field again as the bolt grows, because the bolt itself carries the cloud's charge. D's copper farm needs the field to react to new copper as well. Solving it once gives neither the branching nor the pull toward the tips. Solving it properly costs more, and on a wider world it costs much more. Hence my prototype skips the field.

6. **"Lava-water crust only needs a material flag to stay put" (D's Quench).** Not checked. C's Quench *relies* on the crust crumbling and sinking, because the rubble builds the dam under the lid. So the two Quench designs want opposite behaviour from one rule, and D's "one flag" may become "a second crust material".

7. **"Costs nothing while idle" (said of several concepts).** Mould, Veins, Etch and Tincture never settle, so the renderer's skip for unchanged areas is lost for the whole dish, every frame. That cost is fine, but it has to be quoted from the `ascii` worst frame, not assumed.

8. **Crystals, ice slabs and glass that "carry weight through the existing load rules" (Saltworks, Brine, Fault, High Tide).** Possible, but structural collapse is this project's most surprising subsystem. Every concept that hands a new solid to it should budget a day just for "does it break the same way twice".

9. **Floodgate's "the steam rains on the town a minute later".** This depends on where the weather puts the returning rain. If it falls everywhere, the lesson ("the water you boiled lands somewhere") reads as random rain. Not checked; test it in the 3-day build before building a level around it.
