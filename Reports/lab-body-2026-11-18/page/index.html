One Body

Part two (tighter tethers, below): any tether of 2 or more keeps raid 1 at home, so the recommendation changed. When slime moves away from its body, on a raid or a walk to a new crystal, it crosses the map as a cloud of single walkers. Two rules on the GPU engine make the moving slime keep contact: marchers that wait for the crowd, and marchers tethered so they never step where they would be alone. The surge at the squad is left as it is.

From the model

Most walkers steer by the trail the crowd lays, so they crowd onto the trail they share, and that holds the body. A walker that leaves its body is almost always a scared one: marching on a raid, roaming to food, or panicking. These follow a distance field to their goal and read no trail at all, so nothing ties one marcher to the next. A raid of about 1,700 walkers takes 1,700 separate paths down the field.

The two rules act only on marchers, so the body at rest is untouched. Wait: a marcher walks at (walkers in the 5 x 5 round it − 1) / 6 of its pace, the front waiting for the rear. Tether: a marcher does not step into a cell touching fewer than 2 other walkers; it turns round instead. The 11-12 tries put a tether on every walker on the CPU and split the mould into hunks; this one leaves the body whole (largest piece 97 to 98% in every arm).

The measure: loose walkers

Planet 1, run seed 1, greedy hand. A walker is loose when at most one walker stands among its 8 neighbours. Counted once a game second over all about 12,000 walkers; the body at rest has few, so these are mostly the moving slime. Raid 1 leaves at 0:59 with about 1,736 walkers.

Part two: tighter tethers

Scott asked to see the tether much tighter, so walkers always have to be much closer. Three tighter rules, same scene: tether 3 and tether 4 (the cell a marcher steps into must touch 3 or 4 other walkers), and edge contact 2 (2 walkers, counted only along the cell's four sides, not its corners). A new count says whether the raid still reaches its target: the walkers the squad kills between 0:59, when raid 1 leaves, and 1:16.

What it shows. With any tether of 2 or more, raid 1 does not reach the squad: 20 to 41 kills in the raid's window against 379 with none. A raid leaves the body as a one-wide trickle, and no cell ahead of a trickle touches 2 other walkers, so the marchers stand at the edge of the body. Making the tether tighter only makes that stricter; it does not make a body that travels. Tether 1 lets the raid go (127 kills) but it crosses as dots. A marcher that holds its heading instead of turning round gave exactly the same counts. The calm breakaway is a blob under every tether, and the tighter ones make it no tighter (182 to 218 loose against 192).

Frame cost

Not timed (the software GPU is never timed). The tether reads the 8 neighbours of the target cell once per marcher step; the wait reads 25 cells per marcher. Only marchers pay: about 1,700 during a raid and a few hundred otherwise, against 12,000 walkers in the pass.

Recommendation

Changed after part two. Part one recommended tether 2 for the raid; part two shows it keeps the raid at home, so that is withdrawn. The tether is right for slime walking to food or breaking away: it turns a stream of dots into a blob (loose walkers halved) and costs nothing in play. For a raid it needs the raid to leave as a body first.

So: tether 2 for roaming and breakaway walkers only, not for raid marchers, and a next lab step for the raid, a muster: raid walkers gather at the body's edge until a crowd has formed and then set off together, so the tether has a body to hold. Tighter tethers (3, 4, edge contact) add nothing over 2.
