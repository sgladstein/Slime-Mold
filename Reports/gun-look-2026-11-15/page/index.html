Gun look

A pass on what a shot looks like from the barrel to the slime: the muzzle flash, the bullet in flight, the hit, and the slime it kills. Clips are the game window's own frames on software Vulkan (the Fork, seed 1), before on today's main and after on this branch, one second each at 30 frames a second, with the game's counts under them.

What changed

  • Muzzle flash by gun kind. A white-hot ball at the barrel's mouth with jets in the card's flame colour: the rifle three prongs, the carbine short and wide, the long gun a lance, the scattergun a fan of five. It shrinks over about 4 frames. It used to be a soft disc under the soldier.
  • Recoil. The soldier kicks back as it fires and settles.
  • Tracers glide. The bullet moves smoothly between game steps (15 a second) at its own speed, leaves from the barrel and tapers. The hit lights up when the bullet gets there, white-hot, then gold. A scattergun's pellets share one hit's light, so a fan on one spot no longer whites out the soldier.
  • The spray. Every bit of slime a shot kills flies out of the hit as a droplet, back toward the shooter, each at its own speed, slowing, and dies where it lands. These are the slime's own walkers in the simulation, not a painted effect, and the kills are the same as without it.
  • Thrown slime streaks. Slime a bomb throws, and the spray, draw as droplets stretched along their flight instead of squares.
Every clip loops; slow motion makes the flash and the spray easy to see.

At the slime, zoom 24

The owner's play zoom. Eight soldiers hold at the larder on free fire with plain rounds; the slime comes at them. The two runs are the same game to the shot: the spray only changes how a kill looks.

The mould count holds 3 more walkers after: droplets still in the air when the count was taken. About 80 walkers die a second here and each flies 0.15 to 0.3 s, so roughly 12 to 25 droplets are in the air at a time.

Squad mode, the default game, one squad holding with each gun kind's average gun. The flash is the kind's shape; the long gun's lance and the scattergun's fan read from across the screen.

Bombs, zoom 16

Bomb rounds: a bomb throws the slime round its blast. The thrown slime now draws as streaks along its flight. The pale wash over the ground is the alarm the blasts lay, which this pass leaves as it is.

With bombs the two games drift apart a little by 30 s (+1% kills): a droplet holds its slot for up to 0.3 s, so fewer slots are free for the slime's births in a heavy fight.

The frame is 2.6 ms (4%) slower at the middle; most of it is the GPU drawing the flashes, droplets and lights, and the rest is recording the bullets' glide on every frame. Software Vulkan is many times slower than the 1070, where every frame was under 2.6 ms at the 95th percentile yesterday.

Rules kept

  • The picture is the simulation: the droplets are walkers in the dish; nothing is stamped on the slime.
  • Light is the size of the damage: the muzzle's light is still 1 cell, now at the mouth; a hit's light is its strike.
  • No waves from hits; the camera stays still.

Tried and left out

  • Knocking the living slime back. A first splash threw the slime past the hit and let it live. It cut kills by 30 s from 1,165 to 908 (−22%), and only 1 to 29 walkers flew at a time, mostly over the bright body, so it hardly showed.
  • Screen shake. Noita shakes the camera on explosions. In this game every shot can carry a payload, so the camera would shake through every fight. A small shake on bombs alone is easy to add if you want it.
