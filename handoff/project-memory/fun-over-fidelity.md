Scott (textbook thread, 2026-10-16 18:59): "the overall goal of this work isn't a biologically scientifically accurate ant simulation. It is a fun simulation based game and we are using real biology based on ants and physics as inspiration to make it work. A really cool fun creature that is similar to an ant in a fun world is better than us failing to make an engine that could be published as scientific research."

**Why:** the textbook lane drifted into matching papers to the decimal (sorting ratio bands, Dussutour Fig 10 curve shape, Goss vs Dussutour species conflict), which bought nothing for the game. See [[step-back-2026-10-16]].

**How to apply:** judge rules and world changes by: fun to watch, visible structure for the player (nests, trails, piles, chambers), simplicity, frame cost. Papers supply ideas and a plausibility check, and the *signature* to look for (e.g. "most traffic on the short branch"), never the decimals. Still show a picture and a control; still call weak results weak. Overrides the stricter TEXTBOOK PASS BARS rule in MEMORY.md where they conflict. Related: [[textbook-ant-2026-10-15]].

Scott 19:00 follow-up: "ideally following science makes our task easier, not harder because we have research to follow." So: research is the shortcut — reach for a rule research already found works instead of inventing one; stop tuning once it makes the game better; a paper's rule that fights our world points at a world fix, not at chasing the paper's numbers.
